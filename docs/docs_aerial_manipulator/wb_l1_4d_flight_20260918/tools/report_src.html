0918 — T650-AM Whole-body L1 Adaptive, four-dimensional attribution

First flight of the 4-D joint-row attribution on the real vehicle: 83 s of whole-body DIRECT, five planned legs, every one completed, and the operator returned the vehicle to SAFETY by hand. No watchdog, no saturation, no clamp. The controller is stable; what limits accuracy is the arm's gearbox friction and a 2.2 s motion-capture dropout that the estimator turned into a 15 cm jump.

Findings, most important first

confirmed The controller is stable on hardware and the attribution removes the phantom force

Eighty-three seconds of DIRECT with two arm excursions, a 0.6 m base step and a 15 cm estimator jump in the middle, and every transient decays back to the same bounded wander. Nothing in the actuation chain was near a limit: motor commands in 0.43–0.77, the law's peak arm torque 1.10 of 3.0 N·m, no allocator saturation, no observer bound reached (the 16 N peak was the jump), the arm reference and state fresh on 100 % of samples and the streamed reference on 99.99 %. The force the arm impedance consumed was 0.000 N on all 20 748 ticks; on the 0912 six-dimensional flight the same slot carried a +0.51 N standing bias and peaks of 3.6 N.

defect A 2.2 s motion-capture dropout became a 15 cm jump and a two-tick thrust collapse

From 44.65 to 46.87 s the VRPN feed reported no data. The state estimator kept publishing — 134 of its 210 samples in that window carried a byte-identical position — so the flight node's 1.0 s feedback timeout never fired. When tracking returned, the odometry stepped 151 mm and its velocity read 2.6 m/s for one sample. The law's velocity term turned that into a sideways force command; for two ticks the collective fell to 9 N, the motors to 0.13–0.41, |e_R| reached 1.0, and joint 1 hit its 0.34 N·m duty clamp for 76 ms. The vehicle tilted only to 4.7°, recovered within 30 ms, and re-converged the 200 mm error in about ten seconds, but the next leg was flown with 90 mm still open. Nothing in the controller caused this; something upstream must catch it.

defect The arm moves now, but stops wherever friction catches it

Joint 2 was commanded 40° → 33° → 40° and measured 40° → 35.2° → 35.2°: it never returned. Joint 3 overshoots each move by 2.5–4° and holds the overshoot; joint 4 parks 3° short. The standing joint errors after the first go-home are −4.7 / +2.8 / +3.1° and they set the standing EE error (8–16 mm) and heading error (2.3°). Compared with 0912, where joint 2 rotated 0.35° of a 3.8° request, the friction feedforward on the pass-through is doing its job while the reference moves; what remains is the hold: once the reference stops, that term is zero, and 20 N/m of task stiffness on a 9 mm error is 0.2 N — an order of magnitude under the breakaway torque.

unchanged The slow base wander is the same mode the simulation could not tune away

In every quiet hold the CoM error has a dominant 0.08–0.16 Hz component of 15–35 mm standard deviation, present before the arm ever moved (hover 18/15 mm). It neither decays nor grows and the attitude follows it with 0.3–0.5° of commanded-minus-measured gap. The 18 September twelve-flight sweep concluded this needs a model change, not a gain; the flight agrees.

confirmed The observer's bookkeeping is right, and it is doing the allocator's homework

u1 + d̂z = 36.79 N against a 36.74 N model weight throughout, while the pack drops 0.4 V and the vertical estimate walks from −0.1 to −1.0 N. The allocator still carries the 4.260e-05 experiment thrust constant the 0912 report asked to restore; the flight measures 4.21e-05 → 4.14e-05, so the observer is absorbing a 1–3 % thrust deficit that a re-anchored constant would remove. The −0.10 N·m standing yaw and the 0.5 N lateral bias are real vehicle asymmetries, found and cancelled.

watch The deadbeat reading is twenty-five times noisier than in simulation

The unfiltered momentum-difference estimate has 24/29/15 N of standard deviation on hardware against ~1 N in Isaac, and the raw interaction reading 3–6 N with a 95th percentile of 12–15 N. The 2 rad/s translational filter handles it (0.3–0.5 N), and a collision threshold on the 0.25 rad/s-filtered reading would sit above 0.5 N with margin. But any use of the raw reading — the note's collision latch at collision_threshold_n — must be defined on a filtered value or it will trip on noise.

What to change before the next flight

  1. Catch the mocap dropout upstream of the law. The estimator must not republish a frozen pose as fresh odometry, and the node should treat a single-sample position step above a few centimetres (or a velocity above the planner's v_max) as a feedback fault — hold the previous velocity, or revert to SAFETY through the existing feedback-loss path. Also find why the markers were lost for 2.2 s with the arm folded home.
  2. Give the arm a way to finish a move. Options in increasing order of intrusion: keep the friction feedforward active for a short tail after the reference stops; raise the joint-space authority of the heading and position tasks (K_y 20 → 50, K_ψ 0.3 → 1.0, both flown in simulation); or a small hold-phase breakaway pulse on the arm side. The joint-row trim already finds the residual (±0.18 N·m on joint 2) — what it needs is somewhere to put it.
  3. Re-anchor alloc_thrust_coeff to a measured value and re-derive the SAFETY pair, as the 0912 report already asked; the observer's −1.0 N is the price of not doing it.
  4. Fly the base alone for two minutes to characterise the 0.1 Hz wander without the arm in the loop, and test the observer-predicts-rotor-lag idea from the 18 September sweep against it.
  5. Recover the second bag. The metadata describes a 242 s recording with twice the message count; only its metadata.yaml arrived.
