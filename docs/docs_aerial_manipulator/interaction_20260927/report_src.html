Can the 4-D switch carry pick-and-place and box pushing?

Feasibility of the phase flag χ (wb_l1_contact and its threshold fallback) for the two final interaction experiments, and the payload and push-force limits of the H1b tune. Offline bench on the exact law, five hardware flights for noise, three Isaac flights on the real C++ node.

2026-09-27Plant: 4-D mirror (0918/0921/0924 identified)Gains: H1b, unchangedArm limits: hardware servo caps 0.34 / 2.44 / 1.42 / 0.39 N·m
Q1 · Is the switching logic feasible? What triggers it?
Yes, the attribution and the switch work. It is not usable as shipped: three things need changing first.
  • χ cannot be switched in flight today. The C++ node reads wb_l1_contact once at startup. A gripper trigger needs a small hook in the node; the threshold is the only runtime path that exists.
  • The rendered force is 5 s stale. It shares H1b's wb_l1_omega_x 0.207 rad/s with the free-flight feed-forward. Config fix: reading 2.0 rad/s, feed-forward kept at 0.207 per block. Free flight is unchanged by construction.
  • Use the gripper signal for grasping, a 2 N threshold only as a fallback. The hardware reading's noise floor is 1.3 N (p99.9 at 2 rad/s), so a threshold can never see a 100 g cube (0.98 N). It does catch pushes of about 3 N or more within 0.6–0.8 s.
Q2 · How much can it carry and push?
About 0.3 kg of payload and 6 N of box friction along the arm, with margin.
  • Payload: 100 g uses 53 % of the servo cap. The recommended limit is 0.3 kg (76 %); 0.4 kg is the edge (92 %). From 0.5 kg, joints 2 and 3 saturate and the arm droops.
  • Box push along the arm: up to 6 N kinetic friction (tilt 11°). From 8 to 10 N the push still completes but tilt reaches 15–19° against the 20° watchdog; 12 N aborts. Tilt is the limit, not the arm.
  • Across the arm: about 1 N at most. Joint 1's 0.34 N·m cap and the yaw budget bind first.

How χ works, and the three problems

In the 4-D attribution the task force is read off the four joint rows of the momentum residual, F̂ = Jyq−T([ŵΣ]q − ŵq). The phase flag decides what happens to it. In free flight (χ = 1) the trim ŵq keeps learning the joint rows at 0.2 rad/s, the rendered force is gated to zero, and u3's feed-forward cancels the whole residual. The arm therefore rejects any contact slowly and stiffly. In contact (χ = 0) the trim freezes, the force is rendered into the impedance, and it is removed from the feed-forward. The end-effector then yields by F/Ky, which is 4.7 mm per newton at Ky = 211.9.

  1. No runtime input. gains.l1.contact = wb_l1_cfg_.contact.value is set once in the client; there is no subscription and no parameter callback. The observer does re-read the flag every tick, so exposing a setter is all that is missing.
  2. One scalar serves two jobs. H1b tuned wb_l1_omega_x down to 0.2072 for the u3 feed-forward, because faster rings the 0.94 Hz attitude mode. The same scalar filters F̂f, the force the impedance renders in contact. The shaped task inertia My = 1 kg sits against a natural vertical end-effector inertia of only 0.31–0.41 kg, so a stale F̂ is amplified 2.4–3.3× in vertical contact. The per-block keys wb_l1_omega_x_t/r/q already exist and fix this without touching free flight.
  3. The threshold sits on a noisy, trimmed signal. Before it latches, the free-flight trim absorbs part of the contact. At the shipped 0.207 rad/s it latches 1.5–3.2 s late with 30–50 % already absorbed, and that part is then rejected stiffly instead of rendered.

Pressing a 100 g cube onto desk 2, reference 1 cm below the surface

Desk normal force during a 25 s press. The dashed line at 3.1 N is what the impedance should command: the cube's weight plus Ky × 1 cm. With the 2 rad/s reading and the gripper flag, the force reaches that value about 7 s after the cube touches the desk, then holds it. The shipped reading renders a stale force: over the first 8 s it delivers about half, and it settles near 2.4 N. In free flight the feed-forward integrates the desk reaction as a disturbance, so the force passes 3.1 N after about 22 s and keeps rising. That wind-up is why a rigid contact needs χ = 0.

Isaac, on the real C++ node

Three flights on the 4-D mirror plant, same hover and the same force profile, applied at the grasp point by a new injector (script 07). The profile is 4 N along the arm, 3 N down (a 300 g payload's weight), 1 N across the arm, then 7 N along the arm. The node, PX4, the arm stack and the planner are the real ones. Only the yaml differs between flights.

  • The bench's predictions held on the real node. With the 2 N threshold, χ latched 0.6–0.8 s after each push of 3 N or more and rendered 89–94 % of it. The shortfall is what the free-flight trim absorbed before the latch. With static contact the rendering was 96–103 %, and the deflection matched F/Ky within about 1 mm.
  • Free mode is the risky one for sustained pushes. On the shipped yaml the arm holds the end-effector stiffly while the vehicle pitches up to 12° into the push. It does that by counter-rotating, and joint 3 reached its +50° stop on every push of 3 N or more. After the 7 N push was removed, the arm oscillated for several seconds with joint 3 held on its stop and joint 2 swinging between 24° and 49°. Joint 2's torque reached the law's 3.0 N·m clamp. That is above the real servo's 2.44 N·m, a limit Isaac does not model. With contact rendering, joint 3 stayed at or below 47° and joint 2 at or below 2.05 N·m.
  • Static contact costs a small phantom force. With the trim never running, the arm's calibration residual is rendered as up to 0.46 N while hovering, about 2 mm of end-effector offset. That is why the flag should follow the gripper or the task rather than stay on for the whole flight.

Recommended configuration and task design

Controller (new yaml, free flight unchanged) 
wb_l1_omega_x: 2.0          # wrench reading + contact rendering (was 0.2072)
wb_l1_omega_x_t: 0.2072     # u3 feed-forward keeps H1b explicitly
wb_l1_omega_x_r: 0.2072
wb_l1_omega_x_q: 0.2072
wb_l1_collision_threshold_n: 2.0   # fallback for unannounced collisions

Already written as ..._t650_sim_interaction_thr2.yaml. The hardware yaml needs the same five lines in a new copy. Nothing existing was edited.

  • Pick-and-place: set χ = contact from the gripper-close command until the gripper opens. Carrying the cube in contact mode renders its weight (4.6 mm sag at 100 g) and keeps the pre-grasp trim. Placing then regulates the desk force to weight + Ky × press instead of winding up.
  • Unload before you open. Lift the reference back to the desk surface over about 1.5 s, then open. At 0.3 kg this cuts the release excursion from 88 to 71 mm.
  • Box push: the task flag for the push window, with the threshold as backup. Pushing a sliding box also works in free mode because the box yields, but a stuck box then winds up the same way the desk does.
  • Ramp every force change over at least 1 s. Base excursions come from the pitch moment of an end-effector force 0.24 m ahead of the CoM, and the rotational observer (0.74 rad/s) is what has to absorb it.
  • Geometry check before the flight. At the home pose the grasp point is only 6 cm below the body origin, while the legs reach about 30 cm below it. Grasping on a desk top needs an unfolded arm pose, or an approach from the desk edge.

The gripper hook (not implemented)

A runtime flag needs three small additions in the whole-body fork: a setter on WholeBodyController that forwards to l1_.setGains() with contact changed, a std_msgs/Bool subscription such as whole_body_direct_actuation/interaction_phase (published by the planner or the arm ground station when the gripper closes), and a debug field for the task flag next to [105]. The Python reference already flips contact live, and the parity test would need one more rollout with a flag edge.

A law change worth testing next (not adopted)

Vertical loads move the base because the moment of the end-effector force waits for the lumped rotational estimate at 0.74 rad/s. The 4-D reading already knows that moment at 2 rad/s. Feeding the attributed contact moment to u2 in contact, and only there, is bit-identical in free flight. What these results do not cover

  • No PhysX contact. The payload and the box are force models at the grasp point, and in Isaac the payload is its weight only. The bench has an exact rigid attachment, but friction and grip slip on real hardware are not modelled.
  • The bench arm has smooth friction. Hardware stick-slip makes the frozen trim drift in contact, which the 1.3 N noise floor only partly reflects.
  • Isaac runs at RTF 0.48 on the wall-clock law, with the known L1 artefact. One Isaac flight per configuration.
  • Hardware servo caps are applied in the bench only. Isaac's arm clamps at 3.0 N·m, so Isaac cannot show arm saturation below that.
