AM-T650 simulation comparison · Isaac at real time (RTF 1)

Whole-body L1 impedance vs geometric L1 adaptive vs modular adaptive control

Three controllers flew the same end-effector circle — radius 0.5 m, 24 s lap, the arm's second joint swinging 25 ± 15° on a 6 s period — on one Isaac plant built from identified flight disturbances, one trajectory planner and one SAFETY takeoff/landing loop: our whole-body L1 impedance law; the geometric controller with L1 adaptive augmentation of Cai et al. (CEP 2025); and the modular adaptive law of Yadav et al. (TMECH 2025). Only the DIRECT control law differed, plus, for the geometric law, how the arm is driven: its paper treats the arm separately, so the arm runs a position-mode stack tracking the planner's joint reference, where the other two command arm torques. All results are from Isaac running at real time (RTF 1). The whole-body law flew its shipped configuration, which was tuned on this plant's offline bench; the geometric and modular laws were implemented from their papers and tuned for this plant on the same bench with the same search.

All nine flights ran under one condition: headless Isaac, the real-time pacer holding RTF 1.000, Isaac pinned to the performance cores from launch, the same plant and controller files. EE/base position and EE/base heading are tracking error against the planner's reference; joints are measured minus the streamed joint reference. All nine flights completed the lap without rotor saturation (the geometric law's motor commands stayed within 0.49–0.73); the whole-body and modular flights also had no joint-torque clamp. All nine started the lap through the planner's standard 0.10 m Start gate. The geometric law flew gains tuned for this plant (section 2.3). With its hardware flight configuration it scored 206 mm / 9.60° on the same mission (EE path 0.69–0.70 m), matching its hardware flights of 2026-09-28, and needed the Start gate widened to 0.25 m. The error that remains is structural to the paper's law. Its L1 augmentation acts only on the matched channel (thrust and body torque), so it estimates the body-fixed lateral force, (0.46, −0.45) N, but does not cancel it. The pure-P position loop therefore leaves the airframe F/Kp ≈ 0.74 N / 20.1 ≈ 37 mm off, turning with the heading (measured hover offset 38 mm). With ωd = 0 its attitude damping opposes the circle's 15°/s yaw rate, a (Kω/KR)·rate ≈ (0.41/1.74)·15°/s ≈ 3.5° heading lag that the position-mode arm carries to the gripper, so its EE heading error equals its base yaw error. The whole-body law's own q1/q4 carry the end-effector heading redundancy — it tracks the end effector, not a fixed joint split — so its small per-joint numbers are not directly comparable to the modular law's, which has no such redundancy to spend.

2.3 How the geometric law was tuned

The geometric law was tuned the way the modular law was, on the same offline bench the whole-body tune came from. The bench runs an exact Python port of the C++ node (geometric law, L1 augmentation, ros from the encoders), the reference bridge and Isaac's position-mode arm servo, flying the comparison circle against the mirror plant with hardware-like feedback noise and lag. CMA-ES searched all 11 gains: Kp, Kv, KR and Kω for the horizontal and vertical axes, the two predictor poles and ωc. Each candidate flew the circle at the nominal 16 ms transport delay, at a longer delay it had to survive, and on the stress plant (mass and inertia × 1.10, arm mass and gearbox friction × 1.05, CoM shifted 10/10/5 mm, allocator thrust belief +17.6 %).

Stage 1 used the 28 ms bar the other two laws meet on this bench: the whole-body tune survives 28 ms and aborts at 30, the modular tune survives 34 ms and aborts at 36. Its best set diverged in Isaac. A 1.53 Hz attitude oscillation grew from the switch into DIRECT until the vehicle passed 40° of tilt 19 s later, before the lap began. The bench reproduces that same mode at 30 ms of delay (and for the hardware set at 60 ms), so Isaac puts more effective delay around this law than 28 ms. Stage 2 therefore required 44 ms. The search met it mainly by lowering the L1 filter bandwidth ωc from 6 to 1 rad/s, which keeps the L1 torque correction out of the rotor-lag band near 1.5 Hz; the position and attitude gains stayed close to stage 1. At ωc = 1 rad/s the augmentation compensates disturbances slower than about 0.16 Hz and leaves faster ones to the geometric loop. The tuned set has not been flown on hardware.
