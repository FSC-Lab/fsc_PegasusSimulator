1016 Pick-and-Drop Flights

T650 aerial manipulator, whole-body 4-D L1 controller, EKF2-fused feedback, 16 October 2026. Three bags: one mission without a payload (the control) and two with the 180 g basket. These are the first flights with the 16 October changes: the arm held at the pick pose while carrying, the wrist friction and damping fix, wider arm torque caps, stiffer attitude gains, and the automatic, basket-aimed drop.

The flying is much better than on 15 October, and both baskets ended in the tray. The wrist swing that drove the 15 October oscillation is gone. The two new drop features were not exercised: Drop was clicked by hand inside the automatic drop's 2 s wait, and Ready To Drop was pressed before the aim could measure the basket. Two new problems showed up: j2 sticks under load while the controller winds its torque up, and D-1's gripper closed on the wrong part of the payload.

3. The carry

  • The wrist swing is gone. On 15 October q4 swung ±18° at 0.5 Hz from mid-carry to the drop, and the vehicle pitched ±3–4° with it. Today q4 stays still: its standard deviation over the drop leg is 0.0° in both loaded flights.
  • q4 now parks 3–15° off its 0° reference. The soft EE-heading task gives the wrist only about 0.05 N·m per radian, which does not overcome its static friction. That offset did not move the basket in a way that mattered for the drop.
  • Pitch swing while carrying (0.3–1 Hz rms): 0.21° in D-1 and 0.50° in D-2, against 1.52° on 15 October and 0.23° in today's empty flight. D-2's extra comes from one event, the j2 breakaway 22.5 s after the grasp (section 5).
  • The lift is still the largest transient: pitch reaches ±4–5° within 3 s of the grasp, as on 15 October, and the CoM moves 157–181 mm.

4. The drop

  • D-2: the claw was 59 mm from its target when Drop was clicked, still swinging after the drop leg. It had been 43 mm off at the end of the leg and was moving away. The basket left the jaws 48 mm from the drop point, fell 0.21 m, bounced through the centre and came to rest 30 mm off, flat.
  • D-1: the claw was only 16 mm off, but the basket hung 56 mm behind the claw. The planner's model assumes 20 mm ahead. It left the jaws 85 mm off and came to rest 112 mm off, tilted 25° and 17 mm higher than D-2's basket, which suggests it is resting partly on the tray's edge.
  • Both releases were clean. The basket left the jaws 0.08–0.12 s after the click, moving sideways at only 0.05–0.08 m/s.

5. j2 sticks and winds up under load

  • D-2, from 4 s to 22 s after the grasp, j2 stayed 1.1° below its reference. Over that time the controller's j2 torque rose from 1.25 to 2.44 N·m, its j2 disturbance estimate from −0.54 to −1.50 N·m, and the servo current from 190 to 340 counts. The basket itself accounts for about −0.4 N·m, which is where D-1's estimate sat for most of its carry.
  • At 60.2 s the joint broke loose: the j2 error jumped to +12.7°, j3 to −7.7°, and the vehicle pitched from 1.6° to 4.7°.
  • Heat: j2's worst 10 s mean current was 321 counts in D-2, 225 in D-1 and 244 on 15 October D-1. j2 shut down from overload on 26 August at about 250. No servo error was recorded today.
  • D-1 shows the start of the same pattern in the last 12 s before the drop: estimate −0.34 to −0.75 N·m, current 158 to 226 counts.
  • Why: the gearbox friction grows with the load torque. While the joint is held, the controller's joint disturbance estimate keeps integrating the difference, so it pushes harder, which raises the friction further, until the joint breaks away. The 16 October wrist model predicted the same mechanism on j4; here it appears on the loaded j2.

6. D-1's grasp

  • D-1's gripper stalled at −0.0010, about two-thirds closed. D-2 and both 15 October grasps closed fully, at −0.0096 to −0.0098, on the fin.
  • The claw stood at about the same place relative to the basket in both flights (within 10 mm), so the jaws caught something thicker than the fin. The ground station accepted the stall as "gripped".
  • With that grip the basket hung 56 mm behind the claw and tilted 17–21°, against 8–12° in D-2. That hang is what put it 85 mm off at the release.

8. What to do next, in order

  1. Fly the new drop as designed. Hover at least 6 s at Drop Start before Ready To Drop, then let the automatic drop release, keeping the Drop button as the override. Both features are built and running but have not flown.
  2. Watch j2's current on every loaded flight. D-2 ran above the 26 August overload level for most of its carry. The fix belongs in the arm channel, for example a bound on how far the joint disturbance estimate may grow while the joint is not moving. That needs a decision and has not been tuned.
  3. Check the grip before lifting. A stall far short of the full close (−0.0010 against −0.0097) meant the wrong grip for this payload. The ground station could refuse it, or at least show the gripper position. D-1's video would show what the jaws caught.
  4. The empty wrist still wanders after the release (E-1 −25° to +18°, D-1 −45°). The 16 October model predicted this; the cause sits in the controller, not the arm. It has not affected a drop so far.
