2. The envelope

A 3D-printed core wrapped in red paper, heavy at the far end. The weight is what makes the insert possible. The mailbox is only 115 mm deep inside, so at the release three things must hold at once: the jaw tips stay outside the wall, the far end stays short of the back wall, and the centre of mass is already past the mouth's lip so the envelope falls in when the jaws open. A uniform envelope has its centre of mass at L/2 and cannot satisfy all three with ±20 mm of hover error. Moving the centre of mass to 40 mm from the far end can.

Mass and balance rule

With the end weight centred 15 mm from the far edge and the rest of the mass spread evenly along the 150 mm, the centre of mass lands 40 mm from the far end when end weight = 1.4 × (core + wrap). A full 2 mm PLA core prints at about 28 g at the lab's measured print density (1.04 g/cm³), so print it at 1.5 mm or with lower infill to stay near the 60 g design.

Check before flight: balance the finished envelope on a ruler's edge across its width. The balance point must be no more than 48 mm from the far end. Further from it, the centre of mass may not clear the lip at the worst fore-aft error. Weigh it too: 55 to 65 g is the flown range.

J4's low stiffness does not limit this payload. With the wrist rolled 90° and the envelope gripped by its edge, the envelope's centre of mass lies on the claw's own axis, so gravity barely loads J4. The only J4 load is the weight times the lateral grip offset. A lighter envelope also keeps the lift and release kicks on J2 and J3 small; the 190 g basket kicked q2 by 18 to 25° at its release.

6. Why the mouth is this size

The claw's hold error was measured on 13 hardware flights of this controller (10-09 to 10-21, the claw's forward kinematics on the encoders, odometry pose). The slide-in is not gated, so across and in height the mouth must pass the envelope at the measured worst case. The release and the pick are gated: the arm ground station opens or closes the gripper only with the claw within 20 mm of its target, which bounds the fore-aft error at those moments.

The two thinnest margins are the pick's grip depth (+1 mm: with a 20 mm fore-aft error short of the target the jaws still hold 10 mm of the envelope) and the side and height margins of the mouth (+5 mm). All are positive at the budget; none is large.

7. Running the mission

Arm ground station, Mail Envelope tab, in order

  1. Adjust: the Start row measures where the vehicle actually took off.
  2. Envelope → Get: averages obj_0 resting on the hat (the frozen-feed and flipping-yaw checks apply).
  3. Mailbox: the place point; in simulation it comes from the yaml (1.709, 0.250, 1.024, 0).
  4. Insert Margin: 0.15 m: how far in front of the release the claw waits, level.
  5. Plan: switches the planner to the insert place and plans all six legs.
  6. Go To Start: the take-off hover over the start mark.
  7. Execute To Pick: Ready To Pick (claw 0.10 m behind the near edge), Pick (slides in; the gripper closes within 20 mm and 8 mm vertically), Exit To Pick starts by itself on the grasp and lifts the envelope.
  8. Go To Mailbox Start: carries the envelope to (1.0, 0.25, 1.1) with the arm unchanged.
  9. Execute To Mailbox: Ready To Insert, then Insert (slides in; the gripper opens within 20 mm); Exit To Insert starts by itself on the OPEN report, backs out 0.10 m and climbs 0.20 m; the gripper closes after it.
  10. Go To Land Start → Execute To Land: folds the arm home and lands on the start mark.

Setting the Mailbox point on hardware

Lay the envelope flat on the mouth's lower lip, push it in until its far end touches the back wall, hold it level (its centre of mass is inside, so it would tip in), and press Get on the Mailbox row. The release is 36 mm short of that position and the claw rides 64.5 mm above an envelope lying on the lip, so the place point is Get x − 0.036, Get y, Get z + 0.065, yaw 0. The tab's boxes show two decimals, so set it exactly from a terminal:

ros2 param set /uav_0/whole_body_trajectory_planner pick_place_place_point "[<x−0.036>, <y>, <z+0.065>, 0.0]"

Rounded to −0.04 and +0.06, the claw would sit 4.5 mm low and use up most of the 6 mm room below the mouth. Hover at least 6 s at Ready To Insert before pressing Insert: the descent trim averages the claw's error over 5 s on hardware.

9. Before the first flight

  • Gripper opening. Measure the real fingertip gap when open. The envelope is 4 mm thick and the vertical error budget is ±12 mm, so the open gap should be at least about 30 mm at the tips.
  • Pitch in the foam pads. Hold the envelope by its near 31 mm in the closed gripper and measure how far its far end droops. The design assumes 0 to 20°. More than 20° uses up the room below the mouth.
  • Balance and mass. Balance point no more than 48 mm from the far end; mass 55 to 65 g.
  • Mailbox walls. Measure the real wall thickness and box depth; every distance here is from the outer faces with 5 mm walls. Fix both boxes to the table so the drop cannot move them.
  • Mocap body for the envelope. Pivot at the geometric centre and mid-thickness, x toward the weighted end, z up, markers no taller than 4 mm on the top face between 40 and 90 mm from the near edge. At rest it must read level and steady, with no 180° flips, before Get.
  • Downwash over the box top. The props pass 77 mm over the red box for about 3 s. Watch for a pitch oscillation like the 0.6 Hz one the plastic bin caused on 10-14, and abort if it grows.
