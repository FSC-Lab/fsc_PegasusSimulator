Envelope Mailing Design

Two printed parts for the mail-delivery flight. The envelope core is what the arm carries: a printed PLA plate, wrapped in red paper. The localization frame is how the ground station finds the targets: a plate with five markers on your own holders. Set it on the pick hat or on the mailbox and press Get on that row of the arm GS's Mail Envelope tab; the planner adds the frame-to-target offset itself.

Envelope core what the arm carries

A flat PLA plate, printed in one piece with no inserts, then wrapped in red paper. Four identical rectangular windows make it lighter. They sit nearer the grip than the far end, so the far band is the largest solid area and the far end is a little heavier.

Model it in CAD

Three features

  1. Plate

    Sketch a 150 × 90 rectangle with one corner on the origin. Extrude it 3.8 mm.

  2. One window

    On the top face, sketch a 30 × 36 rectangle with its corner at x 35, y 6. Cut it through all.

  3. Pattern it

    Rectangular pattern of that cut: 2 along x at a pitch of 36, 2 along y at a pitch of 42. That leaves 6 mm webs everywhere and a 49 mm solid far band. Sharp corners are fine; a 1–2 mm corner fillet changes nothing.

What the missing weight changes

From the mailbox and hover-error checks

Without the steel bar the balance point sits near the middle. Nothing in the planner changes, but two task margins shrink. On the hat the near end hangs 70 mm out, so prop wash on it can tip the envelope before the jaws close. At the mailbox, when the jaws open it can tip back out over the lip.

Release margin: the worst of the jaws staying out of the wall and the envelope falling in, with the claw ±20 mm off along the approach. Positive means both hold. “Release 15 mm deeper” means adding +15 to the place offset's dx below. The jaw tips then stop 20 mm from the wall instead of 35.

Build it

  1. Print

    PLA, bottom face on the bed, long edge along an axis, no supports. 0.2 mm layers, 3 walls, 4 top and 4 bottom layers, 20 % infill.

  2. Optional: a heavier far end

    In the slicer, put a modifier box over the far band (x 101–150) and set it to 100 % infill. The part stays the same shape; the balance point moves about 11 mm toward the far end. The table above shows what that buys.

  3. Weigh and balance it

    Weigh it, then balance it across a ruler edge and note the distance from the near edge. It is not critical now, but it tells you which row of the table above you have.

  4. Wrap

    Red paper around 120 g/m², glued to the core, seam on the bottom face. Keep it taut over the windows. Mark the near edge so it goes onto the hat the right way round.

Localization frame how Get finds the targets

One frame with five markers serves both targets. The markers are the standard Ø30 balls on the four holder designs of your valve assembly (taken from Valve_Assembly_v2.stl), each glued into a 2 mm pocket so its height and heading are fixed. Two shallow lips (the skirts) hang under the frame's ends. On the hat their curved middle drops over the platform's rim and centres the frame. On the mailbox their flat ends close on the front and back faces. A U-slot open to one end doubles as the envelope's placement jig on the hat: slide the envelope in until it stops, and it lies exactly where the planner expects.

Build and use

  1. Print

    PLA, skirts down on the bed, supports on the build plate only, under the plate. The underside is the datum, so keep the support's top gap at 0.2 mm. 3 walls, 20 % infill. Needs a bed of at least 210 × 210 mm.

  2. Check the fit

    On the hat it should drop over the rim and not slide; on the mailbox the flats should close on the front and back faces. Sand a face that binds. If it rocks more than about 0.5 mm, lower the clearance CLR in the source and reprint.

  3. Holders and markers

    Print five holders: one each of A, B and C, two of D (holders/holder_*.stl in the zip, or your own CAD). Glue each into its pocket, turned as drawing 3 and the holder cards show it, base pushed down to the pocket floor. Fit the five standard markers as on the valve. Mark the +x arrow on the plate with a pen.

  4. Define obj_0 in Motive, once

    Lay the frame on a flat table with its +x along the room's +x. Select the five markers and create the rigid body obj_0. Keep Motive's DEFAULT pivot, the markers' centroid: do not move it. The planner's offsets start from there.

  5. Pick: on the hat

    Seat the frame on the hat, open end toward the drone's start, +x along its approach. Press Get on the Envelope row. The row now reads the envelope's centre and the frame's heading. Slide the envelope in, far end first, until it touches the stop. Hold it down and lift the frame straight off.

  6. Place: on the mailbox

    Seat it on the mailbox top, +x pointing INTO the mouth, flats on the front and back, ends 17.5 mm in from each side. Press Get on the Mailbox row. The row now reads the release point and the mailbox's heading: nothing to correct by hand. Lift it off. Then press Plan.

  7. Before the flight

    Take the frame out of the field.

Targets and offsets from the frame to the arm

Get reads obj_0's pivot, the markers' centroid. The planner adds a fixed offset, turned by the frame's yaw, and writes the result into the row. On the Mailbox row it also takes the yaw from the frame. The offsets sit in both mail-delivery yamls (hardware and simulation) as pick_place_pick_locator and pick_place_place_locator, and the tab shows them under its points. The claw then goes 60 mm behind the point (EE Offset x −0.06).

StepFrame sits onGet onPlanner adds to obj_0, mTarget from O, mmYaw
X = Xobj_0 + dx·cos ψ − dy·sin ψ    Y = Yobj_0 + dx·sin ψ + dy·cos ψ    Z = Zobj_0 + dz    yaw = ψ
ψ = the frame's yaw, averaged over the Get. The Mailbox row writes ψ as the value nearest the yaw already typed, so a typed turn keeps its direction.

Get refuses a frozen, scattered or flipping obj_0, as before. To fly without the frame, set both parameters to []: Get then reads obj_0 as the envelope itself.

Cut the mouth

—, centred across, top edge — under the top

  1. Find the front face

    The face that looks at the start, flush with the table's front edge. Measure every height down from the top surface, the one the frame sits on.

  2. Centre line

    A vertical line — in from either side. Both sides should give the same line.

  3. Top and bottom edges

    Top edge — below the top. Bottom edge, the lip, — below the top.

  4. Side edges

    Mark them — each side of the centre line. They land — in from each side of the box.

  5. Cut

    Through the front wall only. Keep the lip straight, flat and free of burrs: the envelope slides over it. Square corners, or a corner radius up to 10 mm. Hold every edge to ±2 mm: the pass band leaves 5 mm a side and 5–6 mm above and below.

Field setup where the pillar and the mailboxes go

The designed layout. On the day it may sit elsewhere in the room, as long as the pillar and the mailbox keep their positions relative to each other: the two Gets measure them, Mailbox Start follows the mailbox, and Adjust moves Start, Land Start and Land to where the drone stands. Positions are in the OptiTrack room frame: origin at the field centre, +x forward (the drone's heading at takeoff), +y to the left. Left means the +y side as you look from the start toward the table.
