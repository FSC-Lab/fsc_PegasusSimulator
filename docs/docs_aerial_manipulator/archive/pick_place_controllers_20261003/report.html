1. Performance comparison

Both controllers completed 5 of 5 missions and set the payload upright on the place cap. On the Suarez et al. benchmark metric the whole-body controller kept the airframe 4.1× closer to its planned position during the grab (max εUAV 46.9 vs 189.9 mm) and 2.2× closer during the place (59.9 vs 133.8 mm). The decoupled controller reaches comparable accuracy at the gripper by moving the airframe more and taking longer.

1.1 Metric and protocol

Suarez et al., "Benchmarks for Aerial Manipulation", IEEE RA-L 5(2), 2020, Eq. 9:

‖εUAV‖ = ‖rUAVref − rUAV‖ ,    ρUAV = ‖εUAV‖ / L ,    L = L1 + L2 = 0.370 m
  • rUAV is the airframe (body origin) position in the Earth frame, from odometry. rUAVref is the planned airframe position, rebuilt from the planner's stream as xcd − Rz(ψd) r0c(qd) on the planner's own model. It is the same for both controllers, which fly one planner. In hover the CoM error and the airframe error agree to 0.4 mm.
  • L is the arm's maximum reach: shoulder to elbow 0.130 m, elbow to claw 0.132 + 0.108 m. So 0.1 L = 37 mm and 0.25 L = 93 mm.
  • Protocol (the paper's grasping benchmark, Sec. IV-B): N = 5 missions per controller, interleaved (whole-body, decoupled, whole-body, …), each from a clean simulator. A mission is the arm ground station's Pick & Place steps, scripted: Go To Start, Ready To Pick, Pick, Exit To Pick, Go To Place Start, Ready To Place, Place, Exit To Place, Go To Land Start, Execute To Land.
  • Phases: approach = Go To Start to Ready To Pick; grab = Pick to Exit To Pick (descent, close, lift-off); carry = Go To Place Start to Ready To Place; place = Place to Exit To Place; return = Go To Land Start to Execute To Land.
  • Success: the mission is not aborted and the payload ends upright (< 5°) on the place cap, its centre within 80 mm of the pillar axis. Way-point condition: ε < 0.25 L, averaged over ±0.2 s at each of the 8 leg ends. t10%: time from the lift-off (payload 5 mm off its cap) or from the release (gripper open command) until ε ≤ 0.1 L and stays there for 1 s.
  • Scene, planner block, plant and feedback are identical for both rigs; only the controller and its arm mode differ. One decoupled attempt aborted 3 s into DIRECT because of a race in the test driver at bring-up: a takeoff setpoint reached the planner after the mode switch. The driver was fixed and the run re-flown; the aborted attempt is not counted.

1.5 Findings

  • The large errors are horizontal and follow the payload's weight change. In the 12 s after the lift-off the decoupled airframe peaks at 150–297 mm from its reference, against 46–53 mm for the whole-body airframe; in the 10 s after the release it peaks at 120–159 mm against 54–66 mm. Neither law models the 200 g payload. The whole-body law's coupled model and L1 observer bring the airframe back inside 0.1 L within about 2 s; the decoupled law takes 7.5 s on average.
  • The decoupled airframe sits about 50 mm off its reference at rest (approach RMS 49.5 mm against 5.0 mm). Its position loop is pure P/D. The plant carries a body-fixed lateral force of (0.55, −0.50) N that its L1 estimates but does not cancel, because that force is outside the matched channel. The loop therefore parks the airframe at |F| / Kp = 0.74 N / 15 N/m ≈ 50 mm. That is above 0.1 L, so its t10% is reached only where the payload's weight happens to shift the offset; after the release 2 of 5 runs never got inside 0.1 L.
  • Accuracy at the gripper is comparable. The jaws closed with the claw 0.9–10.1 mm (whole-body) against 2.2–8.4 mm (decoupled) from the target, and the payload was set down 9.6–24.2 mm against 4.4–15.7 mm off the pillar axis. The decoupled rig gets there through the planner's descent trim, which measures its 32–56 mm hover offset and shifts the descent by it, and through the centring wait before the jaws close. That makes its grab and place phases about 6 s and 4 s longer and its mission about 11 s longer (112.8 against 101.9 s).
  • The whole-body pick is world-anchored by design. During the pick descent the claw is held in the world frame and the airframe absorbs the hover wander, so part of the whole-body grab-phase ε is intended.
  • Attitude. Peak tilt was 3.9–4.6° for the whole-body controller against 5.0–15.2° for the decoupled one. The worst decoupled run (bench_geo_1) swung 297 mm at the lift-off and tilted 15.2°, just above the planner's 15° guard but for less than its 0.1 s persistence.
  • Scope. Simulation only: Isaac Sim with the plant identified from hardware flights, raw motion capture, one payload mass, five runs per controller. Neither pick-and-place configuration has flown on hardware.

2.3 Decoupled geometric + L1

Geometric SE(3) control of the airframe with L1 augmentation (Cai et al., 2025), and the arm in position mode tracking the joint half of the same plan. The horizontal Kp / Kv are softened from the free-flight 20.11 / 11.05 because, carrying the payload, that pair left a lightly damped ~0.6 Hz roll and pitch sway that grew to the tilt guard; a damping screen picked 15 / 10. The vertical pair is raised for the lift.
