1024 Box-Pushing Flights

Six flights: three in the morning with the new q3 guard and close gate, three in the afternoon after re-defining the box's mocap body. Why does the safety guard still trip, why do the jaws miss the fin, and what comes next?

Afternoon (A, B, C): the aim is fixed, the hand-over is not. With the jaws on the fin the claw now sits 1 mm from where the planner aims (35 mm in the morning).

Every guard trip was real. Each time, the arm was being pulled toward its q2 wall at 40–65°/s; q3 bottomed at 6–12°.

The pull comes from the hand-over at the end of Ready. The claw is 28–36 mm short of the fin along the push, and the planner holds it in the room anyway, so the arm has to make up the gap. The base is short because of a 3–5° pitch error, which the law turns into 0.7 mm of position error per mrad along the nose.

The jaws "miss" because they never close. 28–36 mm short puts the claw at the fin's back end, outside the 25 mm close gate. In B and C the guard aborted first.

Next: keep the claw on the vehicle until it is within about 15 mm and slow, then hand it to the room hold.

Morning (#1–#3): the new guard and gate worked. #1 failed on a 35 mm aim error (re-placed markers), and #3's box was blown off the table by the downwash.

2Afternoon: why the guard trips and the jaws miss

The aim is fixed

All three flights used EE Offset (0, 0, 0.183). In A, with the jaws on the fin, the claw sat −1.1 mm across from the mocap origin; in the morning it was −35 to −38 mm. The re-defined box body puts the fin where the planner aims.

When Ready's 3.2 s move ends, the planner switches the claw from following the vehicle to a fixed point in the room, blended over 1 s. The free-axis anchor ramps in over the same second and takes up only part of the along-push error. So for that second the arm has to close the gap itself: q2 rises and q3 falls. In C the base was 33 mm short and still; in B it was swinging back 65 mm in 1 s. The guard tripped at q3 17–18° in both. q3 then bottomed at 9–12° with q2 at 41–43° (#2: 6°). The guard was right to stop it.

The morning's #1 had the same 36 mm shortfall, and its arm barely moved. Its base happened to be moving forward at the hand-over and closed the gap by itself within 0.7 s. Same software; the phase of the vehicle's swing decided it.

Why the jaws "miss" the fin

The gripper's close gate waits until the claw is within 25 mm of the grasp point, within 8 mm across, and slower than 30 mm/s. In B and C the claw never got that close before the guard aborted, so the jaws never closed (the finger stayed at +19 mm). The fin is 60 mm long along the push, so a claw 28–36 mm short sits at the fin's back end. Closing there would have caught the end of the fin or nothing; the gate was right to wait. In the morning's #2 the miss was the 35 mm aim error instead.

Why the base is short: a 3–5° pitch error

The CoM's along-push error follows its pitch attitude error, at 0.56–0.71 mm per mrad (correlation 0.84–0.95). The law's structure predicts 0.71–0.75: thrust over the position gain, about 36 N / 50 N/m. The attitude loop has no integral, so a pitch moment it does not know parks the attitude at moment / k_R, and the position loop then parks at that tilt times thrust / k_x.

Pitch errors of 60–95 mrad (3.4–5.4°) appear twice per approach:

  • At the end of Go To Start. The arm finishes unfolding and the vehicle stops; the base runs 45–68 mm past its point.
  • During Ready's 150 mm descent. The base falls 30–45 mm behind.

Each is a change of 0.15–0.25 N·m in the pitch moment. The fast estimate inside the observer sees it at once; the filtered estimate the law uses follows 1.5–2 s later (ωc,r 0.74 rad/s). Where the moment comes from is not in the bag: the arm unfolding during the final approach (the valve study's mechanism), and something during the descent above the table. Ready also starts only 1.6–2.2 s after Go To Start, while the base is still 15–49 mm past its point.

Flight A: the push stuck, lurched, and the release came mid-swing

  1. The grasp survived. q3 dipped 12.7° and the jaws closed 1.5 s after Ready, with the claw 7–12 mm from the grasp point.
  2. The push stuck for 3.5 s. q2 and q3 stood exactly still (zero velocity) from 48.0 to 51.5 s while the law raised j2's torque from 0.89 to 1.33 N·m: joint stiction. The arm-side friction feed-forward follows measured velocity, so it gives a stuck joint nothing. Meanwhile the vehicle drifted 27 mm back and dragged the box 22 mm back with it; the base fell 82 mm behind its reference.
  3. The guards nearly tripped, on the wrong reading. The observer booked the stuck joints as a 4.6 N contact force, against a box that slides at about 0.6 N. The force guard reached 4.7 of its 5 N. The box-lag guard read only 22 of 40 mm, because its free-axis correction excused about 60 mm.
  4. Then the joints broke loose at about 60°/s and threw the box 190 mm forward at up to 240 mm/s (planned: 21 mm/s). The push ended at 154 of 150 mm with the vehicle swinging 65 mm sideways and about 110 mm along the push.
  5. The release came mid-swing. The automatic Exit started 0.24 s after the push, while the base moved back at about 100 mm/s. The jaws take about 0.5 s to open, so the clamped arm stretched: q3 26 → 7°. The guard tripped at q3 17°.

3What to change before the next flight

  1. Settle before the room hold (planner code change). After Ready's move, keep the claw following the vehicle, and hand it to the room only when the claw is within about 15 mm of the grasp point (8 mm across) and slower than 30 mm/s for 0.5 s. Wait up to about 6 s; if it does not settle, stay on the vehicle and say so. The arm then has to make up only a few millimetres, not 30. The arm GS's 2.5 s close-gate timer must start at that hand-over, not at the end of Ready.
  2. Start Ready after Go To Start's overshoot has settled. Start the automatic Ready on the claw's error and speed rather than after a fixed 1.0 s (push_pull_auto_ready_delay_s), or raise the delay to about 3 s as a stop-gap.
  3. Release only when the vehicle is calm. Start the automatic Exit on the base's speed and CoM error, not 0.2 s after the push.
  4. The push's stick-slip needs its own fix. A stuck arm gets no help from a friction feed-forward driven by measured velocity, and the box guards read the stuck phase as normal. Try in Isaac first, with the hardware-like drift model and joint stiction.
  5. Then reduce the pitch-moment change itself. Unfold the arm before Go To Start's final straight run, not during it. Hover 10 s at the Ready-end pose with the jaws open, to see whether the moment stays (an effect near the table) or decays.
  6. Do not loosen the q3 guard. In every afternoon trip the arm was being pulled into its q2 wall at 40–65°/s. Remove the pull.

4Morning: were the changes valid?

The close gate closed early and calm

On flight #1 the gripper closed 1.49 s after Ready, with the claw 14 mm from the aimed point, 7.5 mm across it and moving at 13 mm/s. The old 3 mm gate would have kept waiting. The open-jaw hold before the close was the calmest of all three hardware pushes:

The q3 guards behaved correctly

  • Flight #1: silent; q3 never went below 14.2°.
  • Flight #2: fired on a real fast fold, q3 15.3° falling at 47°/s. q2 was at 39°, 6° short of its stop, so the new q2 credit correctly gave nothing.

One weakness the flight exposed

The gate's speed check looks at the claw. While the claw is held in the room, the arm absorbs the drone's motion, so the claw read 13 mm/s while the drone was already drifting sideways at 20–40 mm/s. A check on the drone's speed would have held the close about 0.5 s.

5Morning: why #1 looked less stable

The fin was 35 mm from where the planner aimed

While the jaws hold the fin, the claw is on the fin. So the planner's model claw minus the grasp point it computes from the box's mocap pose (obj_0 + EE offset) measures where the fin really is:

The model error is a few millimetres (10-21, 10-23). On 10-24 the aim was 35 mm off.

Which way. "Across" is the box's x axis, the jaws' closing axis (the fin is a 5 mm plate whose faces point along x). The box sat at yaw −0.1°, so box x is room x. The drone took off at x −1.47 and hovered behind the box with its nose toward −y, the push direction. The planner aims directly above the mocap origin (EE Offset 0, 0, 0.183). The real fin was 34.8 mm toward room −x of that point: the near side of the table, toward the take-off spot, which is the drone's right while it pushes. The fin did not move on the box. The markers were re-placed, so the mocap origin moved 35 mm toward the far side (+x) of the fin.

Only the across offset is measured. The closed jaws centre the 5 mm fin across, but the fin is 60 mm wide along the push and tall, so any shift of the origin along the push or up is not visible from the grip. The box's height is unchanged: z 0.8498 on 10-24 against 0.8508 on 10-21. The 0.8704 on 10-23 came with the table at another place (box at x 1.31), so it says nothing about the markers.

What happened, in order

  1. Hold (47.52–49.02 s). The claw held still, 24–26 mm beside the real fin, the near pad next to it.
  2. Drift starts (from 48.85 s). The drone began sliding sideways at 20–40 mm/s; the wrist rolled 4 → 10° to keep the claw still.
  3. Close (49.02–49.65 s). The pads pulled the claw onto the fin and dragged the 240 g box 38 mm sideways.
  4. After the grip. The drone swung out to 104 mm, then back to −91 mm by 52.7 s; the clamped box stayed about 80 mm out. The roll attitude error reached 136 mrad, and one rotor pair hit 0.00 twice (49.05 and 49.60 s).
  5. Abort (52.41 s). The clamped box was dragged sideways and tilted 6.3°; the box-tilt guard aborted 1.26 s into the slide.

6Morning: flights #2 and #3

#2: the claw missed the fin

The claw arrived about 12 mm from the aimed point, which with the 35 mm aim error put it about 47 mm beside the real fin, outside the jaws. It also drifted 6 → 43 mm short along the push during the hold. The room hold stretched the arm (q2 rising at 30°/s, q3 falling at 47°/s) until the rate guard aborted 1.2 s after Ready.

#3: the box was blown off before the descent

At the end of Go To Start (43.4–44.2 s) the box slid 15, then 46, then 122 mm, flipped and fell to the floor. The drone was hovering 0.46 m above it and 0.36 m behind it, at the same place as on flights #1 and #2. Nothing noticed: the GS started Ready 2 s later toward the captured grasp point, and the operator aborted at 47.89 s. The box guards act only with the claw on the box.

8Morning recommendations

  1. Fix the aim (done for the afternoon flights: the fin is within 1 mm). Best: move the box body's pivot in Motive back onto the fin's plane, directly below the fin as before. Quick fix for the present markers: EE Offset x = −0.035 m (box frame; the sign follows from the measurement, fin = origin − 34.8 mm along box x). The quick fix corrects only the across offset, because the along and vertical offsets of the new origin were not measured. Placing the markers as in Box_Assembly_Push.stl makes the layout repeatable, but it does not set the origin by itself. Motive puts a new body's pivot at the marker centroid, and for that layout the centroid is 10 mm across, 20 mm along and 30 mm above the box centre. The planner's EE Offset (0, 0, 0.183) is measured from the box centre, so with an unmoved pivot the claw would aim 10 mm beside the fin and 5 mm above its top. Either move the pivot to the box centre, or keep the centroid and use EE Offset (+0.010, −0.020, 0.153) in the simulation's box axes (the signs depend on how the body's axes are set in Motive; the sizes do not). Before each session, put the claw on the fin by hand and compare the planner's model claw with Get's grasp point: they must agree within about 5 mm.
  2. Check the box before Ready and before the close. Refuse if the live box moved more than about 10 mm or tilted more than about 2° since Get. That would have stopped flight #3's descent toward an empty table.
  3. Downwash. Flight #3's box was blown off at the same hover point as #1 and #2. Use the heavier box (500 g target) or hover higher or further back before Ready.
  4. Add the drone's speed to the close gate. Check its strictness on the three hardware holds first.
  5. Fresh pack per push, or check at least 24 V before Go To Start.
