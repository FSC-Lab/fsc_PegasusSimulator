What the data show

  • The push along the turn is small. The valve needs about 0.45 N at the fin to turn. Along the nose that makes almost no yaw.
  • Something pushed the claw to the right, 1.5 to 3 N by 34.0 s. Two independent estimates agree on the direction: the controller's own force estimate, and the yaw the rotors delivered against the vehicle's clockwise turn (divided by the claw's 0.21 m lead on the centre of mass).
  • The claw could not move left. Joint 1 stayed at −0.4° while its torque rose to 0.5–0.6 N·m, and the arm's target ran 70 mm to the claw's left.
  • That push twisted the vehicle clockwise. The rotors' counter-clockwise yaw climbed to 0.75 N·m: the CCW pair hit idle at 33.7 s, and the allocator then raised every motor to keep the yaw, until all four were on a limit at 33.9 s and roll and pitch were lost.
  • The fin turned relative to the U: the valve turned +34° while the vehicle turned −8°, about 41° between them by 34.2 s.

What the bag cannot show

  • Which surface of the claw the fin touched. At this depth the U leaves 4.8 mm on each side of a centred 5 mm fin (Isaac). The model's claw point is off by 8–16 mm at a similar pose in Isaac, so the close-up cannot settle it.
  • The most likely contact. A rightward force on the claw, with the fin on the claw's right, fits the fin pressing the inside of the U's right rail. A fin turning counter-clockwise inside the U swings its back end to the right, into that rail.
  • Correction to my earlier reply. I said the U's rear leaves about 20 mm. Isaac says about 15 mm. A fin turned more than about 13° cannot lie inside it along its full 40 mm length, so the contact was at a corner near the U's base.
  • The yaw-balance estimate is a model. It uses the allocator's thrust map and the bench yaw constant; the 09-26 identification put the real yaw response at 0.43–0.78 of bench, so the true force is likely nearer the controller's estimate.
