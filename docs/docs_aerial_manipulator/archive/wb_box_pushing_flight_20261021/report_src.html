1021 Box-Pushing Flights

T650-AM, whole-body 4-D L1 on raw mocap240 g box, 0.10 m push at 21 mm/s5 bags, 2026-10-21

Result

  • The box was pushed 107 mm (97 mm at rest) and stayed upright. Vehicle tilt stayed under 5.7°, with no actuator saturation.
  • The exit was not the normal Exit. The planner's q3 guard tripped 0.78 s after the push ended and ran an abort. The vehicle was then flown home and landed.
  • The new mocap guard caught every real mocap fault (8 VRPN pauses, 1 drone flip) with no false abort.
  • 500 g / 0.50 m: not yet. In contact the arm swings about twice as much as in Isaac, and the swing already used the whole q3 range.

What happened, in order

  • 36.5 s. The claw reached the handle and was held in the room (the planner's world anchor). Until then the arm had stayed locked at 32.2°/29.9°. From here on it absorbs the base's motion along the arm, as a q2/q3 antiphase swing. Isaac shows the same swing at half the amplitude.
  • 38.9 s. The jaws closed, 2.4 s after arrival. The swing was already 6° rms.
  • 39.6–41.1 s, the settle. The clamped box was pushed 10.5 mm backward before the push began.
  • 41.1–45.8 s, the slide. The box stuck for ~1.8 s, then surged at ~100 mm/s to catch up. q3 reached its 50° stop and q2 its 45° limit.
  • 45.84 s. The push ended. The contact flag went off, but the jaws stayed closed: the ground station's automatic Exit was due 1.0 s later.
  • 46.62 s. q3 fell to 5.0°, below its 10° guard, and the planner aborted. The jaws opened within 30 ms. The abort planned from the measured pose, so it held the arm buckled (q2 on 45°, q3 ≈ 2°, −7.3° at worst) for ~10 s while climbing 0.30 m, then folded it home. The vehicle stayed calm (tilt ≤ 3.5°).

Two more findings from the same flight:

  • The box's mocap body mis-solved during contact. At 40.85 s it reported a 96° tilt and a 61 mm jump for 3 samples. Only the box guards' 0.2 s persistence kept that from aborting the push.
  • The claw sat below its reference. Under the world hold it was 8–10 mm low (Isaac 0–3 mm), so the arm lifted the box by about 1 N. The cause is joint stiction that the arm's feed-forward had learned while the joints were stuck (item 9).

4. Friction

The bench calibration (μ 0.25) is good enough; this flight cannot refine it. Averaged over the slide, the law's force estimate reads about 0.25 N of push resistance, while the arm was also lifting the box by about 0.8 N. That gives μ ≈ 0.16 ± 0.1: consistent with the bench's 0.20–0.28, but less precise. (In Isaac the same estimate reads within 0.1–0.2 N of the true contact force.) The box's stick-slip came from the arm swing, not from friction: the clamped box moved 10 mm backward before any push started.

5. Next steps

Before the next push: configuration, lab and procedure

  1. Release sooner after the push ends config
    Set the arm GS's push_pull_auto_exit_delay_s from 1.0 to about 0.2 s. Today's trip came 0.78 s after the push ended, and the swing's half-period is ~0.9 s. Once the jaws open, the arm stops absorbing the base's motion. This removes the window the trip happened in, but not the swing; a release during a swing is so far tested only in Isaac (6 of 6).
  2. Measure the real table into push_pull_table lab + config
    The hardware yaml still carries Isaac's table (x 0.600–0.750, top 0.80 m). The planner refused the box at x 0.576 and accepted it at 0.637. Its edge check and its landing-gear clearance both use this box.
  3. Re-define the box's rigid body in Motive lab
    Use an asymmetric marker set. The body mis-solved 3 samples in contact (96°, 61 mm) and ~10 times at 15.7–17.9 s, with the vehicle 1.7 m away. A 24 s push gives five times today's exposure.
  4. Exercise the mocap-fault abort once on hardware flight test
    Trigger the estimator's mocap_guard/test_fault (0.5 s) in a hover away from the table. No bag reached CONFIRMED or LOST, so the abort path has only ever run in Isaac.

Before 500 g / 0.50 m: simulation and code

  1. Damp the arm swing while the claw is held in the room sim campaign → config
    This is the main item. The swing is 10.5° rms on hardware vs 5.5° in Isaac. It used q3's full 5–50° range, touched q2's limit, and drove the box's stick-slip. Isaac reproduces the mode, so candidates can be ranked there first: more damping on the EE task, a partial world hold in contact, a shorter world-held wait before the jaws close. Already rejected in Isaac: EE damping D_y 40 and CoM damping k_v 20/25, which left DIRECT in free flight.
  2. After a joint-guard abort, fold the arm off the buckled pose planner code + Isaac test
    Once the jaws are open, drive the arm toward the push pose instead of holding the measured one. Today's abort held q3 at about 2° (−7.3° at worst) with q2 on its limit for ~10 s. The Isaac jammed-box case can test it.
  3. Gate the box's mocap samples before the box guards use them planner code
    Reject a sample that jumps more than 20 mm or 10° from the last good one, as the drone guard does. Today a 25 ms mis-solve reached both box guards and the push monitor.
  4. Clean the velocity after a mocap pause or flip estimator or processor code
    Use EKF2's velocity while the guard blends back, or stop the processor republishing a frozen pose. After the flip, a 3.75 m/s spike gave 1–2 ticks of +61 N thrust command. It was harmless in free flight today; in contact it also kicks the arm torques.
  5. Keep the arm's feed-forward from learning stiction (why the claw sits 8–10 mm low) law code, low priority
    The cause is found; the joint trim is not it. From 28.9 to 36.9 s, with the claw riding the CoM, q2/q3 did not move (std 0.01°). Static friction held them, and the observer booked that friction as a joint disturbance: +0.15 N·m on j2 and +0.11 on j3. The arm's feed-forward learned it through its 10 s filter (wb_l1_omega_x_q 0.1) and subtracted it from the command. The world hold set the joints moving at 37.3 s. Within ~1 s the friction no longer held them, but the feed-forward kept subtracting ~0.1 N·m on each joint, so the arm sagged. Rebuilt from the logged residual, this predicts −9.2 mm; −9.3 mm was measured. The trim cannot explain it: the offset appeared 2 s before contact, and in free flight the trim does not reach the control law. After the grasp the box (2.35 N) held the claw where the jaws closed. The offset is harmless for this grasp. Two untested fixes: clear the arm rows of the feed-forward when the world hold starts, or give those rows a faster filter. Isaac cannot test either, because its friction model has no stiction.
