What the sweep shows. The geometric law's peak error follows the peak yaw rate, not the size of the 8. A = 0.60 m at 0.15 m/s turns at 48°/s and peaks at 115 mm, about the same as A = 0.75 m at 0.20 m/s (51°/s, 121 mm). At the same speed a smaller 8 is worse: A = 0.70 m at 0.20 m/s turns at 55°/s and peaks at 128 mm. Slowing down is what helps: at 0.10 m/s both sizes peak at 73–81 mm. The whole-body law stays at 14–24 mm rms and at most 49 mm peak at every point. Every flown candidate fits the 2 × 2 m square for both controllers once the predicted flight peaks are added, but the geometric law's margin shrinks from 169 mm per side (A 0.60 m, 0.10 m/s) to 19 mm (A 0.75 m, 0.20 m/s).
