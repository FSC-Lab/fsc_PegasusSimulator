Valve Resistance Test

Should the valve resist the turn with a hanging payload on a drum, and how much torque should it resist? Six simulated flights compare no load, a hanging weight at 0.10–0.40 N·m (with and without a one-way ratchet), and a rotary damper.

Do not use

Hanging weight: makes the turn worse and is unsafe without a ratchet.

Every weight from 0.10 to 0.40 N·m roughly doubled the peak tilt (9.9–11.5° vs ≤ 6°), and 4 of 5 weighted turns tripped the 10° safety guard. Without a ratchet, opening the jaws let the weight spin the beam back 552° at up to 1060°/s.

Promising

Rotary damper, 0.5 N·m·s/rad: the best turn on record (one flight).

Base lead 22 mm (38–58 unloaded), arm on a joint stop 4 % of the turn (9–13 %), less arm swing, the same tilt, and nothing stored to spring back at release.

Target

About 0.1–0.2 N·m during the turn, from speed-proportional damping.

That is 0.6–1.25 N at the 0.160 m handle radius. A damping coefficient near 0.5 N·m·s/rad gives it at the turn's 12–25°/s; 2.0 was too much in an earlier stress run.

1. What was simulated

Every flight is the shipped valve-turning mission: the whole-body 4-D L1 controller, the arm pose [0, 5, 25, 0]°, a planned 360° turn in 37.5 s (peak 25°/s), the 10° tilt guard, headless Isaac at real time, raw mocap feedback, and the valve's measured-bound bearing friction of 0.048 N·m. Only the valve's resistance changes from run to run.

  • Hanging weight. A constant torque m·g·r that resists the turn's direction. The string starts just taut with the payload on the floor, takes the full weight after 3° of winding, and goes slack if the drum unwinds past the start. It stays on whenever nothing holds the valve, so it back-drives the valve the moment the jaws let go.
  • Ratchet. A one-way pawl: backward rotation is stopped and the pawl carries the weight.
  • Rotary damper. A torque proportional to the valve's speed, in both directions, zero at rest.

3. Why the weight fails

During the turn: the tilt doubles and the arm swings

  • The weighted turns start like the others and fall apart in the middle or the end of the turn: the tilt grows over a few seconds until the guard trips (at 145°, 269°, 335° and 355°).
  • An arm-yaw / wrist-roll swing of about 1 Hz grows before the trip. In ld_w020, q1 swung −12…+22° and q4 −25…+12° between 12 and 18 s; over the same window the unloaded turns kept q1 within −8…+3° and q4 within −5…+9°. In ld_w010r the same swing grew in the deceleration (q1 to +18°, q4 to −18°).
  • The arm swings toward its elbow-singular branch. Two of the five weighted turns took q3 below 5° (1.0° at 0.40 N·m, −3.0° in the second 0.20 N·m run); no unloaded turn went below 9.7°. At 0.40 N·m the arm then buckled through q3 = 0 within 0.5 s of the abort and the vehicle flipped.
  • A lighter weight does not fix it: 0.10 N·m tripped the guard at 355°, in the turn's deceleration.

At release: the weight spins the valve back into the claw

  • 0.20 N·m on the 2.6×10⁻³ kg·m² rotor (plus the payload's own inertia) is about 70 rad/s². The handle moves about 25 mm back within 0.07 s of the jaws loosening and over 200 mm within 0.2 s, much faster than the vehicle can climb clear.
  • This happens at every release: the planned exit, an abort, or a dropped grip. On the real rig a falling payload would also swing on its string.
  • A ratchet holds the valve at release (ld_w020r_relfirst held at 360–361° after its turn), but it does nothing for the turn itself.

4. The damper

The damper resists only while the valve moves, in proportion to its speed. At 0.5 N·m·s/rad that is about 0.22 N·m at the turn's 25°/s peak and zero at rest. It absorbs energy instead of storing it, and there is nothing to spring back when the jaws open. In this flight the valve also turned most evenly: its speed varied by 7.5°/s (std, 8–18 s into the turn) against 9.3–10.7°/s unloaded and 10.1–13.2°/s with a weight.

The useful window is bounded above. An earlier stress run at 2.0 N·m·s/rad (about 0.9 N·m at peak speed) aborted on the 5 N handle-force guard. So the range to explore is roughly 0.25–1.0, with 0.5 the one point flown.

5. What to build

  • A two-way rotary damper on the valve shaft, about 0.5 N·m·s/rad: 0.1 N·m at 12°/s, 0.2 N·m at 25°/s. Choose a silicone-fluid (viscous) type with no one-way clutch.
  • Your gear idea fits here. With an n:1 step-up the damper sees n times the speed and needs only 1/n² of the coefficient. At 3:1 that is about 0.056 N·m·s/rad, roughly 12 N·cm at 20 rpm on the damper side, a common size for small rotary dampers.
  • Bench check before flying: hang a weight on a string at the handle (r = 0.160 m) and time the turn. 130 g (0.20 N·m) should pull it round at about 25°/s. Here the weight is a calibration tool, not part of the experiment.
  • Keep the payload-lift idea only as a demonstration, if at all. It needs a ratchet, and even the lightest load tested here (0.10 N·m) doubled the tilt. Nothing below 0.10 was tested.

6. Limits and next steps

  • One flight per design. The unloaded valve turn completes about 6 of 9 times in this simulation, so a single flight cannot rank designs alone. The weight finding stands on five flights that all doubled the tilt. The damper finding stands on one.
  • Next: two more damper flights at 0.5 and one each at 0.25 and 1.0 (about 20 minutes) before buying parts. Also measure the real bearing's breakaway torque; it is at most 32 mN·m and likely a few.
  • Model limits. The payload's own inertia is left out, so a modelled back-spin is about 15 % faster than real. The ratchet model holds during and after a normal turn but leaks slowly after an abort. The simulation's Coulomb friction cannot make a valve stick, which is why friction brakes were not tested above 0.048 N·m.
  • A harness defect found and fixed during the test. Since 10-19 the shared mission driver defaults to the box-pushing exit (CoM anchor before the jaws open), and the valve harness silently flew it. That exit is known to fail on the free valve handle, and it caused the two exit failures marked in the table. The harness now requests the valve's exit by name, and a baseline flown with it completed as on 10-18.
