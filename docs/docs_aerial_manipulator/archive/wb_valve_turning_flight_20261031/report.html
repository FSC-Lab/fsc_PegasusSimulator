1031 Valve-Turning Flights

Three flights in the afternoon, one in the evening after the fixes. None grasped the handle. Section 6 records every problem and what has been fixed since.

Afternoon: the wrist roll (joint 4) swung ±40° from the start of Ready, so the jaws never closed. Joint 4 had no damping left in the arm controller. Two defects, both fixed before the evening.

Evening: the fixes worked and the wrist held. The failure moved to the base. With the claw held in the room, the base drifted 22 cm sideways in 1.5 s and the arm took all of it, until the elbow straightened and folded through q3 = 0. No guard was armed during the Ready descent; the operator aborted, and the vehicle reverted to SAFETY at 25° of tilt and landed.

Why the base ran: not an external force. The roll loop was already three times worse than in the afternoon by the end of Go To Start, and in the room hold, with the arm still, a roll torque the law does not model appeared (0.125 N·m in the observer). Its source is not identified: the evening flew two changes at once. The fold itself is structural: a claw held in the room at a near-straight arm has 2–3 cm of reach to spare.

Fixed since (1 Nov): before the grasp the planner now watches the base, not only the claw. Ready waits for the base to settle. A 40 mm drift in the room hold hands the claw back to the vehicle. A 60 mm drift on the descent aborts. The arm and sway guards are armed from the room hold. In Isaac every run with the shipped limits completed without a false trip, and each protection acted cleanly when forced. Replayed on the evening data, they act 0.3 s before the elbow straightens. But the flight node takes 1.0 s to hand the claw back, so that hand-back must get faster before the next flight. Not flown, not committed.

2Afternoon: the wrist had no damping

In the pass-through the arm controller cancels the motor's own back-EMF damping (1.28 N·m·s/rad on joint 4) on purpose. Two things are meant to remain: the controller's error damping (d = 0.05, i.e. 0.064 N·m·s/rad) and the law's soft EE-heading task. Two defects removed the first.

  • The current trim integrated the error damping away. The damping is delivered as a deliberate current. Joint 4's 0.2 Hz current trim read it as an error and cancelled it: 80–95 % on the flight data, 93 % on the mock-hardware loopback of the real controller.
  • The mission's damping raise went to the wrong controller. The Valve tab raises joints 1 and 4 to d = 0.2, but it sent the raise to the station's controller parameter, which the hardware station declares as position_controller. All three flights logged "not reachable".

What tipped it: joint 4 breaks free at about 0.045 N·m and the law's hover noise already reaches 0.03–0.04. The room hold's start moved the heading target 5–10° within 0.5 s, and once sliding nothing damped the wrist.

The evening build fixed both (the trim's target now includes the damping current; the raise goes to push_pull_valve_arm_controller) and added a grip schedule: stiff heading (Kψ/Dψ 2.5/0.93 plus the joint-4 stuck hold) while the jaws are open, the valve's soft 0.2484/0.2903 once they close, with the heading-radius term counted from the grasp. These were checked on the arm loopback and the ground-station harness. Neither has an airframe in it.

3Evening: the wrist held, the base ran

Every fix did what it was built to do:

  • The arm logged the raise, j1..j4 error damping [0.20 0.00 0.00 0.20], and the damping duty tracks the joint speed at 0.22 / 0.26 N·m·s/rad on joints 1 / 4. The trim no longer removes it.
  • The grip schedule ran: interaction set at DIRECT entry (the jaws were closed from the landing), free-flight set once Go To Start opened them, heading-radius term off before the grasp.
  • The wrist did not swing. q4 followed q1, and the heading error stayed within 2.6° until the arm buckled.

Why no guard fired

The planner's q3 guards (static and rate) and its sway guard engage only when the claw is at the handle or the turn is flying. Ready, the descent onto the handle, is neither. Its 10° tilt guard is armed, but the tilt passed 10° only after the operator's abort.

Replayed with the flown values from the start of Ready, the q3 rate guard would have fired at 23.56 s, 0.82 s before the operator, but with q3 already at +4.8° and falling at 58°/s. The static and sway guards would have fired 0.2 s later, at q3 −16 to −19°. None of the three would have tripped in the afternoon Ready phases. So the guards would catch this fold at the singularity, not before it.

Changed after this flight: the guards are now armed from the room hold, and the base itself is checked before and during Ready (section 6).

4Why the base ran

Not an external force

The force on the base that the law did not command, computed from the mocap motion (mass times acceleration minus the commanded thrust and gravity), is flat through the onset: within ±0.1 N of the −1.5 N sideways bias it carried all flight. PX4's attitude estimate against the mocap is steady at the onset. The hold geometry is the same as the afternoon's to the millimetre. The sideways acceleration came from the law's own thrust direction: the vehicle sat about 1° rolled the wrong way for 1.5 s.

The roll loop was already worse before the room hold

Roll attitude error, standard deviation over the last 3 s of Go To Start: 39 mrad this evening against 6–15 mrad in the three afternoon flights. In the evening's own hover before Go To Start (jaws closed, soft heading) it was 12 mrad, like the afternoon. So the evening differs from Go To Start on. That is the phase in which both new things were active: the stiff pre-grasp heading from 8.7 s and the damping raise from 9.95 s. The valve also stood in another part of the room.

A roll torque appeared with the arm still

From the room hold (22.17 s) to 22.9 s the joints moved less than 0.2°. The arm's error damping acts only on joint speed, so it was zero. Yet the base drifted 54 mm sideways in that 1.2 s hold. Over the same time the observer's estimate of the roll disturbance rose 0.125 N·m (afternoon: at most 0.05) and the attitude error reached 162 mrad. A roll torque the law does not model appeared, and the observer learns it slowly. At 23.4 s the law wanted −6° of roll and the vehicle sat at +1°.

The room hold turned the run into arm motion

With the claw and its heading held in the room, every base motion becomes arm motion: sideways into q1, yaw into q4, and the quarter of the along-nose motion the free axis does not absorb into q2 and q3. At the turn pose the elbow is 2–3 cm from straight. An 11 cm sideways run adds √(0.224² + 0.11²) − 0.224 = 2.6 cm of reach, and the 8 cm backward run about 2 cm more. That puts q3 through 0 at 23.64 s, as measured.

In the afternoon the undamped wrist swung ±40° instead. It looked worse, but it kept the base decoupled from the arm.

Ruled out

  • The yaw channel. The law's yaw torque averaged 0.29 of the free budget in Ready, the same as the afternoon (0.28–0.30). The rotor allocator traded thrust once, at 23.91 s, after q3 had crossed 0.
  • The arm's error damping as the trigger. It is zero while the arm is still, and the drift began with the arm still. It, and the stiff heading's larger joint-1 torque (0.57 against at most 0.28 N·m), may have fed the growth once the arm moved. The data does not show that.

Not identified

The source of the roll torque. The evening flew the stiff pre-grasp heading and the raised damping at once, in another part of the room, and once. Candidates: the new heading gains acting through the law's arm-reaction compensation while static friction holds the joints (the law cancels a reaction the stuck arm never delivers), flight-to-flight variation (the 17:49 flight's Go To Start reached 30 mrad over its whole length), or the room position.

5One problem, four routes

Every hardware valve contact or room hold this week ended with the arm out of its range within 2–8 s, each time by a different route:

  • 10-28: false tilt-guard trips cut both turns; a growing 0.56–0.6 Hz sideways sway sat underneath.
  • 10-29: that sway reached the arm's limits and the abort turned it into a crash.
  • 10-31 afternoon: the undamped wrist swung ±40°, and the jaws never closed.
  • 10-31 evening: the damped wrist held, and the base ran away.

The common cause: a claw held in the room at a near-straight arm pose has 2–3 cm of reach to spare, against a hardware base wander of 7–8 cm peak to peak. Fixing one channel moved the failure to the next. The afternoon's fixes were checked without an airframe in the loop.

The new protections, replayed on the evening flight

  • 23.15 s: the base leaves the gate's 30 mm. From here Ready is refused.
  • 23.33 s: the leash acts (the CoM error 56 mm), 1.16 s into the hold, with q3 at 14.2°. The claw starts back to the vehicle.
  • 23.41 s: the flown Ready. The gate refuses it twice over: the room hold is released, and the base is 68 mm off and moving at 116 mm/s. The descent never starts, so the descent guard does not come into play.
  • 23.56 s: the q3 rate guard, now armed from the room hold, aborts at q3 +4.8°. Nothing trips it earlier.
  • 23.63 s: q3 reaches 0 in the flown run.

The weak point is the hand-back. The node ramps the claw's anchor over 1.0 s, so when q3 reached 0 the ramp was 30 % done and most of the drift still went into the arm. A 0.25 s ramp would have finished just before q3 reached 0. That is the next change (row 7). A faster ramp moves the EE reference by up to the drift within it, so it needs its own Isaac test before it flies.
