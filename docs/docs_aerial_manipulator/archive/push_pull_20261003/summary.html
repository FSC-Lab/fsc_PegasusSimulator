1Tracking errors and evaluation metrics

eimp = Fext − (Md ėv + Dd ev + Kd ey)

Impedance residual, per channel (x, y, z in N; heading in N·m); 0 means the commanded impedance is realised exactly. Fext is the true wrench on the claw from PhysX's contact report on the box (normal + friction), ey the true claw position minus its reference, all terms low-passed at 5 Hz. Check on every run: in the steady slide Fext equals the table's friction on the box (e.g. 1.05 N and 1.05 N).

εUAV = ‖rUAVref − rUAV‖,   ρUAV = εUAV / L,   L = 370 mm

Platform deviation, as in the pick-and-place benchmark: the planned airframe position against the true airframe position; L is the arm's maximum reach.

HOLD · SLIDE · RELEASE

HOLD = from the claw's arrival on the handle to the start of the slide (grip + settle, the box held by static friction). SLIDE = the 12 s move. RELEASE = push end to Exit (jaws open).

Inside the real arm's q2 range, pushing and pulling (latest)

Your measured limit, q2 between −20° and +45°, is now a hard stop on q2 in the scene. q3 keeps its +50° stop and must stay above 0°: below that is the elbow-singular branch, where the arm folds the wrong way and the controller loses it. All runs in this section use the T650's shorter gear (skids 0.2425 m below the body, a copy of the asset), raw mocap and the gripper as the contact switch. A pull starts the box at y = −0.25 m and draws it 0.50 m back.

What limits the arm. The claw is held fixed in the world, so the arm takes up every millimetre the airframe drifts from its plan: 4–5.5° of q2 and 6–8° of q3 per centimetre along the arm, whatever the fold. The fold only decides how much drift fits before a joint reaches a limit:

What reduced the drift. The controller's translational disturbance observer (bandwidth 2.93 rad/s) keeps re-estimating the box's reaction while the box sticks and slides, and pushes against it. At the 50° and 40° folds with that observer as shipped, the drift was 15–29 mm (std) and every run failed. With it slowed to 1.0 rad/s the drift was 7–11 mm, and both 50° pushes stayed inside the joint limits (pl_71, pl_74). The two slow-observer pulls still failed, for a different reason (Priority 1). Freezing the observer in contact instead made the airframe sit 55 mm off its plan from the moment of the grip (pl_67). The drift's dominant frequency varies from 0.15 to 1.5 Hz between runs, so the observer is a contributor, not the only source.

The end-effector reference is parallel to the table. Over the 0.50 m slide the planned claw height varies by only 1.34–1.42 mm in every run; the planned CoM height is exactly constant. The small change is not a planner error. The arm is held still while the planned airframe tilts about 0.1° to accelerate, which raises and lowers the claw's 0.25 m lever. An exactly flat claw path would need the arm to counter that tilt during the push.

The redesign: post handle and folded arm

Your suggestion, tested: move the handle to a vertical post on the box's top centre (the push line through the box's friction centre) and fold the arm forward so the claw sits closer to the vehicle's CoM. The post must then be taller so the landing gear clears the table. The contact phase also switches on the gripper: on at the jaws' grip, off when the push ends, before the jaws open.

2Key issues and what to fix

Ordered by priority. Each item states the evidence from the runs above and the next step; "untested" marks proposals that have not been flown.

Priority 1Inside the real q2 range, the airframe's drift in contact is larger than the arm can absorb

With the claw held fixed in the world, the arm must take up the airframe's drift. Inside q2 ≤ 45° and q3 ≥ 0° that leaves 1.3–4 cm, depending on the fold. In contact the shipped controller lets the airframe drift 10–29 mm (std). Results: only the 50° push with that observer slowed to 1.0 rad/s slid the box the full 0.50 m inside the range (2 of 2; pl_71 completed, pl_74 failed after the jaws opened). Every other push inside the range failed (0 of 4: 70°, 50°, 40°, and 50° with the observer frozen), and no pull succeeded (0 of 6). In each failure the drift drove q2 to 45° and q3 below 0° (or, at 70°, pinned q3 on its +50° stop), and the force guard or the watchdog ended the run.

Next steps
  • Fold the push pose to [0, 26, 24, 0] (50°), the most balanced joint window (3.3 / 3.5 cm), with the translational observer at 1.0 rad/s. Its cost: the observer is global today, so free flight converges more slowly too; the approach error rose from about 0.05 to 0.08 of the reach.
  • A contact-only observer bandwidth: keep 2.93 rad/s in free flight and drop to ~1 rad/s only while the gripper holds the handle. This is a small code change in the whole-body client. Untested.
  • Pulling: no configuration pulls inside the range yet (0 of 6). At 70° with the asset's 50° stop, one pull completed with the force within 6° of parallel (pl_73: residual 1.20 N, ρ 0.067); there q2 happened to peak at 42°. The same pull with the 45° stop failed when the drift reached q2 = 45° (pl_76). In both 50° pulls with the slow observer, the box ran ahead of its reference at mid-slide (0.3 m/s against ~0.09 m/s planned) before the arm unfolded. Why it runs ahead is not yet explained.
  • The planner still allows q2 up to 50° (a compiled limit shared by every mode). It plans no q2 above 45° here, but nothing stops a plan from asking for it. Make it a parameter before the experiment.
  • Confirm the 45° figure on the real arm: whether it is the airframe in the way or the servo limit, and whether the arm hits it softly or hard.

Priority 2The controller flies on the wrong state while it is in contact

In contact, PX4's attitude drifts 1–3° (mostly yaw) and EKF2's base position drifts 9–15 mm, so the controller places the claw several millimetres from where it is and the impedance pushes on that phantom. This is the one problem the redesign did not touch: with the post design, fused feedback still completed 0 of 6. The approach knocked the box twice (Priority 4), the force guard tripped three times with real contact spikes of 15–17 N in the stick-slip, and one run left DIRECT. On raw mocap the same design completed 6 of 6.

Next steps
  1. Hybrid feedback for contact tasks: position and attitude from the motion capture, velocity from EKF2. Needs an estimator or relay change; the controller's odometry topic is hard-coded. Untested.
  2. Planner and controller on the same attitude (the planner computes the grasp target and its trim with PX4's attitude).
  3. Read the Pixhawk's EKF2_EV* before flying. At the 0.1 defaults EKF2 treats the mocap as 10 cm accurate; noise mode 1 with about 0.01 / 0.03 / 0.05 halved the contact drift in sim (pl_36). PX4 still floors the vision position noise at 1 cm.
  4. Bench check on the vehicle: grip the box and log PX4 attitude and EKF2 odometry against OptiTrack.

Priority 3Interaction geometry: the post handle and folded arm work; the force is still not parallel to the table

Adopted as the scenario default (70° fold, post top 0.298 m, grasp 0.273 m), before your q2 limit: at 70° q2 reaches 47–50° in the push, so that pose does not fit the real arm (Priority 1). On raw mocap, against the side fin:

  • Slide impedance residual 2.5 N → 0.94–0.98 N (60° fold: 1.27–1.68 N).
  • ρUAV max 0.18–0.22 → 0.06–0.09.
  • Net pitch moment on the vehicle 0.18–0.22 → 0.00–0.12 N·m; push tilt 9–10° → 3–6°.
  • Claw slip on the handle about 21 mm → 2–4 mm; box yaw up to 9° → −1 to +2° with the gripper switch.

What did not improve: the contact force points 28–38° above the table (the claw presses down 0.55–1.0 N, more than the side fin's 0.4–0.55 N). Only about 0.4 N of it is the vertical impedance error; the rest comes from the grip on the post. The 70° fold barely changed it (expected 0.36 of the push, measured about 0.6), so the claw angle alone does not explain it. It costs little torque today, because the claw now sits ahead of the CoM and the upward reaction offsets the horizontal push's moment, but it loads the box onto the table (normal force 4.5–5.0 N instead of 3.9 N), which raises the friction to overcome.

Next steps
  • Find where the vertical force comes from: per-contact data on the post (jaw pads against fingertips) is already in the recordings.
  • Trim the grasp height so the vertical impedance error is zero. Consider a handle face the palm meets horizontally. A fully horizontal claw (90° fold) needs a 0.35 m grasp and fails the tipping rule for this box. Untested.
  • Your real handle: a post on the box's top centre, 20 mm across the jaws, top about 0.30 m above the table (scene default).

Priority 4The landing gear can hit the box; the planner only knows the table

At the grasp the skids straddle the 160 mm box with 6 cm to spare each side, but hang below its 95 mm top. Approaching at grasp height, a skid clipped the box and pushed it 45–52 mm (pl_40, pl_41; measured skid 1.6 cm from the box side, 6 mm below its top). The planner's keep-out covers only the table top. Separately, from the old 1.0 m takeoff the planner refused Go To Start (gear vs table, pl_37).

Fixed for now, and the proper fix
  • The approach starts 0.12 m behind and 0.10 m above the grasp, the exit climbs 0.20 m, and the mission takes off to 1.3 m (all in the push yaml and Command.md §17).
  • Proper fix: a keep-out for the box and its handle in the planner, from the captured box pose and its known size. Untested.
  • The scene now flies the T650's shorter gear (skids 0.2425 m below the body instead of 0.3125, a copy of the asset; the original is unchanged), and the planner's gear depth matches. The vehicle rests 0.235 m high and lands to 0.24 m.

Priority 5The gripper as the contact switch

Implemented as two planner options and made the default: CONTACT on at the Push press (sent on the jaws' grip), free when the push completes, before the jaws open. Raw, release residual 1.07–1.35 N against 1.70–4.11 N with the old timing (pl_39 spiked to 11.6 N as the jaws opened in contact); box yaw stayed within ±2°. On fused feedback it is not enough on its own (pl_47, pl_48 failed).

Priority 6Static friction at the start of the slide

The box breaks loose only once the claw trails its reference by F / Kd ≈ 5–6 mm. In the attitude-fix failures it stayed stuck for about 3 s with the tilt already 5–6°, then broke loose in bursts. Real box base: equal static and sliding friction (PTFE glides or felt, not rubber). A force-led start (pre-load before the reference moves) is untested.

Priority 7Gains are mostly not the lever

Thirteen gain and timing variants across raw and fused feedback (observer bandwidths, contact reading, arm and heading stiffness, base and arm damping, push speed); none beat the shipped configuration and several made it worse. The geometry change did more than all of them together. One exception appeared inside the real q2 range: slowing the translational observer to 1.0 rad/s is what keeps the joints in their window (Priority 1).

Priority 8The evidence is thin, and part of it exists only in simulation

  • 1–4 runs per configuration, and outcomes vary run to run. Repeat ≥3 times before relying on a number; the q2-range results are 1–2 runs each.
  • Fext ground truth exists only in Isaac. On hardware the residual needs a force/torque sensor at the gripper, or the controller's own contact reading (about 25× noisier on the 0918 flight than in sim).
  • The simulated jaws, pads and post are idealised; the real grip on a post may carry the push differently.
  • The scene's console table is 150 mm deep and the real box 160 mm wide (5 mm overhang a side); the real box mass is assumed 400 g. No hardware push-and-pull configuration exists yet.
