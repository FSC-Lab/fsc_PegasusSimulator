Valve-turning vs Box-pushing logic

The box push that worked on 10-27 (tag box-pushing-20261027-flown) lets the claw follow the vehicle all the way down, holds it in the room only after the planner sees it settled on the handle, and closes the jaws at once: 0.46 s of room hold before the grasp. The valve-turning task now runs the same workflow, button for button, on the branch valve-isolation-20261102, except the inside of the exit: the valve opens the jaws with the claw still held in the room, because its handle cannot move sideways. The valve now never closes the jaws on the fin: the open gripper is a U, and the bracket between its claws pushes the fin's back edge around the turn. For that the claw holds 80 mm under the fin top and 25 mm behind its centre, the turn is planned 93° for a 90° valve turn, and the planner's path-following governor holds or aborts a turn whose valve lags. An ABORT climbs at once, since the jaws are already open, with the claw following the vehicle. Flown end to end in Isaac, not yet on hardware. The 11-01 valve flight, which held the claw in the room from the end of Go To Start, is in the Archive at the end.

3 · How the 10-27 box code does it

  • Planner (fsc_trajectory_planner at the tag, 7d8ede9). When Go To Start ends it holds the claw in the room only if push_pull_world_anchor_ready is true; the box yaml sets it false. When Ready ends it starts the hand-over settle (push_pull_world_anchor_on_handle: true, settle tolerance 0.025 m, 0.015 m across, 0.030 m/s, 0.3 s). Every 10 Hz arrival tick compares the claw (arm kinematics on the encoders, placed with mocap position and PX4 attitude) with the grasp point; when the settle holds it asks the flight node to hold the claw in the room.
  • Flight node (built at 53877d3). The anchor service sets a target; a weight blends between "follow the vehicle" and "held in the room" over 1 s. The law's claw reference is the planned point plus that weight times the base's CoM error, plus the free-axis shift: once the room hold starts, 75 % of the base error along the nose still passes into the claw reference.
  • Arm station (a0cb8b8). Ready starts by itself 1.0 s after Go To Start, opens the jaws and descends on the open report. Push waits for the hand-over, then closes on its gate (25 mm, 8 mm across, 30 mm/s, 2.5 s limit) and pushes on the grasp result. Exit starts 0.2 s after the push, once the base is calm (40 mm/s, at most 3 s): release, open, climb.
  • Contact comes on at the Push and goes off at the end of the push or at the release. Nothing reads the jaw position.

4 · What still differs, and why

  • The inside of the exit. The box releases the claw to the vehicle before opening, because the box slides and a world-held open claw made the arm absorb the base's drift (10-17). The valve opens with the claw held in the room and keeps it held for the first 0.12 m of the climb, because its handle cannot move sideways: a claw that follows the vehicle presses an open finger into the fin (Isaac 10-18: 2 of 7 release-first against 3 of 3; 10-28: 2 of 6 lost DIRECT without the room-held climb, 7 of 8 clean with it). The U push's claw reaches 80 mm down the fin, so the room hold now lasts 0.12 m (was 60 mm). The free-axis anchor still lets 75 % of the base's motion along the nose pass to the claw, so the arm only takes the sideways part. The start conditions are the box's, plus the base-error wait.
  • The U push. The box clamps its fin, because a box is pushed by friction and must not be dropped. The valve's handle is bolted to the table: a clamped claw is fixed to the world, and every error of the base then loads the arm. The valve never closes the jaws: the bracket between the claws pushes the fin, and the claw slides off it if the vehicle backs away or climbs. That only works with a deeper grasp, 80 mm under the fin top and 25 mm behind its centre: at the old 25 mm the bracket sits above the fin and the valve turned 19° of 90 in Isaac. Closing even part way at the deep grasp puts a claw on the fin (half close: a wedge; full close: a 10–12 N clamp and a tilt-guard abort). Isaac on the bare fin: 5 of 5 plus the end-to-end check, the valve at 85.5–95.1° for a 90° target, tilt ≤ 3.4°.
  • The abort. The jaws open at once; the vehicle climbs the moment they read open, not after a fixed window, in one 0.30 m climb (1.5 s), then folds the arm home; the claw follows the vehicle. With the U push they are already open, so the climb starts at once. The box keeps its 10-27 abort (1.5 s release, then the climb).
  • The governor. A U only pushes: if the vehicle falls behind it leaves the fin, and if it loses the fin it can fly into it (Isaac, the old grasp: the landing gear hit the fin at ~70° of lag). The planner's path-following governor slows the turn from 10° of valve lag, holds it at 20° and aborts after 6 s of hold; it never acted in the nominal runs and aborted a turn that could not push with nothing touched. The box push has no governor.
  • Task numbers, not workflow. Approach height 0.10 m (box 0.15), exit climb 0.20 m (0.25), the grasp pose and depth, the 93° turn for 90°, the close gate's 20 mm and heading check, the translational observer bandwidth 2.0 (box 1.0), the heading target that turns with the handle, the guard thresholds (q3 3° vs 10°, a sway guard, a rotor-tilt guard).
  • Valve-only safety additions. The node's watchdog asks the planner for an abort before reverting to SAFETY (your 10-31 design), the arm is released at a SAFETY handover, and the jaws are closed again after the Exit or an abort.

Archive · The abort that crashed, 11-01

The sway guard aborted the 11-01 static-hold test 0.82 s after the jaws were closed by hand. The abort's plan was the usual one: hold the Ready pose 2.0 s while the jaws open, climb 0.30 m straight up in 3.95 s, fold the arm home in 3.82 s. Two settings made it a crash. The claw's reference was handed to the base: within 0.25 s it was the planned claw point plus the base's own CoM error, so the claw went wherever the base swung, at fin height. And the hold was a rest point that the base, already moving at about 0.4 m/s, overshot by 153 mm and swung back through at about 0.5 Hz. On the swing back the open claw came back at the fin's end and knocked the valve from 8° to 19°. At the start of the climb it caught the fin and dragged it while the base swung away; 1.1 s into the 3.95 s climb, with the reference only 28 mm up, the tilt reached 25.6° and the node handed the vehicle to SAFETY.
