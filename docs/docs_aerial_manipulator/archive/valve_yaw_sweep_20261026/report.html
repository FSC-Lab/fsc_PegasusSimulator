Valve Heading-Channel Sweep

Can a 90° valve turn in 12 s, with a stiffer heading impedance and more valve friction, make the end-effector impedance's heading (torque) channel show clearly, and at which friction should the rubber pads be cut? The sweep crossed the heading stiffness Kψ 0.25–10 N·m/rad with valve friction τf 0.05–0.25 N·m.

No cell

No safe cell made the heading couple visible.

The plan's test was a mean couple of at least 0.07 N·m resisting the turn and at least twice its spread; no run passed it. The best mean was +0.09 N·m ± 0.12, in a Kψ 3 run that broke three safety checks. There is no lookup table and no pad target to give.

Cause

The claw twists on the fin, so the grasp, not Kψ, sets the couple.

In every turn the claw's heading moved 10–45° relative to the beam. The grip's torsional stiffness about the vertical was 0.1–0.8 N·m/rad, below every Kψ tried and below the tangential channel's K·r² = 3.07 N·m/rad.

Unsafe

Kψ ≥ 2 aborts even at the lowest friction.

A stiffer heading channel drives the ~1 Hz q1/q4 swing into the platform's yaw. Its frequency rose from 0.92 to 1.23 Hz, the yaw torque reached 78–112 % of the free budget, and four aborts ended with q3 buckled or the vehicle inverted. Kψ 1 was the one stiffer gain that flew clean, and slightly better than today's 0.25.

Fixed

The scene's valve friction could not hold the valve; a stick-slip model now can.

At 0.9 τf the old model crept at 8–20 °/s, and it never delivered more than ~0.65 N·m per rad/s. The new PEGASUS_VALVE_FRICTION_MODEL=drive holds still at 0.9 τf and slips at 1.1 τf. It is off by default, and every run here used it.

1. What was simulated

Every flight is the shipped valve-turning mission, changed only where the plan asked:

  • Controller and arm: the whole-body 4-D L1 controller, arm pose [0, 5, 25, 0]°, x/y/z impedance 120 N/m and 20.2 N·s/m.
  • Turn: 90° in 12 s after a 1.5 s settle, then 3.5 s on the fin.
  • Exit: the valve's own exit, which opens the jaws with the claw still held in place, then climbs.
  • Guards: 10° tilt, 5 N force, the q3 elbow guard, and the node's 15° watchdog.
  • Simulation: headless Isaac at real time with raw mocap.

Only two things changed from run to run:

  • The heading impedance: Kψ, with Dψ = 2.6 √(0.05 Kψ) for a damping ratio near 1.3 and Mψ 0.05 kg·m².
  • The valve's Coulomb friction τf.

A diagnostic column also turned off the free-axis anchor, a controller option shipped on 10-22 (rows tagged “anchor off”).

4. Why the couple does not show

The valve only fixes the sum of the two moments about its axis. The plan assumed the claw sits rigidly on the fin, which would make the split Kψ/(K r² + Kψ). It does not sit rigidly.

  • The claw twists on the fin. Isaac's true base yaw plus q1, minus the beam's yaw, moves through 10–45° in every turn (std 3–15°); a rigid grasp would hold it constant. Against that twist the couple gives an effective torsional stiffness of only 0.1–0.8 N·m/rad. The simulated claw holds the fin between near-point finger contacts, which behave almost as a hinge about the vertical. The heading channel therefore mostly steers the claw's twist, and the couple stays at the grip's level, about ±0.1–0.2 N·m.
  • The free-axis anchor adds an offset. It moves the claw's position target with the base's drift along the tangent, but not its heading target. With it on, the tangential force over-drives the valve (Ft r 0.04–0.21 N·m) and the heading spring holds the claw back, so the couple comes out negative. With it off, the couple averages about zero and Ft r carries the whole load.
  • Raising Kψ stiffens that twist against the platform's yaw. The swing frequency went 0.92 → 1.0 → 1.08 → 1.23 Hz for Kψ ≤ 1, 3, 6 and 10. The claw's twist std grew from 3 to 15°, and the yaw torque reached 78–112 % of the free budget at Kψ ≥ 2.

5. What would work

  • Do not cut the pads for heading visibility yet. With this grasp, more friction only moves load into the tangential force.
  • Make the grasp hold a couple first. On the bench, measure the real gripper on the fin: torque about the vertical against twist angle. It needs to be well above Kψ, several N·m/rad. A pin-in-slot, a wider two-point fin or a keyed handle would provide it.
  • Grasp on the axis. With a knob or T-handle at r ≈ 0, Ft r vanishes and the whole valve load must pass through the couple; the plan's own formula then gives a share of 1. This still needs a torsionally stiff grasp.
  • Keep Kψ ≤ 1 on this rig. Kψ 1 flew slightly better than today's 0.25: tilt 5.0° against 5.9–7.2°, yaw torque 49 % against 63–67 %. That is one run each and not yet confirmed.
  • For any future valve-friction study, use the stick-slip friction model.

6. Limits

  • Most cells flew once. This rig scatters widely from run to run: the two reference runs differ by 1.3° in peak tilt and 0.10 N·m in mean couple.
  • No hardware-like drift model was used, and nothing was flown on hardware.
  • The grip's twist stiffness is the simulation's; the real foam pads are unmeasured.
  • The plan's growth check (the swing in the last third of the turn against the first) fails by construction, because the swing starts from rest. The verdict used the last third against the middle third and the hold instead; both ratios are recorded.
  • The 90°/12 s reference itself reached 7.2° of tilt once, against the plan's 7° limit.
