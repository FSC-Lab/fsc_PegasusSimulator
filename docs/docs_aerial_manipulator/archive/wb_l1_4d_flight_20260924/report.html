Overall. All four flights flew the compatible 0.5 m circle under the whole-body law with zero rotor saturation and zero joint clamping. Flights 2, 3 and 4 completed their lap and returned to HOLD. Flight 1 was cut short at 1.57 laps by a 2.85 s pause in the motion-capture stream, which the fused estimator swallowed as a frozen, valid position; the law then pushed the real vehicle 1.35 m along the tangent, and it touched down 3.7 m away after the pilot took over (section 4.1). Tracking is similar in all four flights (section 2): the base holds the circle to 4–6 cm rms, the end-effector's absolute error is essentially the base error, and the arm itself adds about 1 cm. The arm joints are the weak channel (section 3.1). On the slow morning sinusoid they lagged and parked at 15–72 % of the commanded swing. On the faster afternoon designs they swing through the full range, but in stick–slip bursts that overshoot, because the whole-body pass-through has no joint-space loop to hold the friction feed-forward honest. The torque the servos apply matches what the arm controller intends to 1–5 %. The configuration changes (Ethernet, CPU isolation, 400 Hz, EKF fusion) are measurable and mostly positive; the 400 Hz raw gyro has a cost worth knowing (section 4.2).

The circle in 3D, all four flights

End-effector error by channel

The end-effector error has two parts. The absolute error is the measured EE (forward kinematics of the measured base and joints) minus the planned circle. The task error is what the whole-body law regulates: with wb_ee_anchor_com the EE reference is held about the actual CoM, so the task error excludes the base's own tracking error. The two are related exactly by absolute = task + base CoM error; the logged signals satisfy this to 0.5–0.8 mm rms against the planner's independent FK.

  • In x and y the absolute EE error is the base error. The blue and grey traces lie on top of each other in every flight (switch the task error off in fig. 2 to see it); the arm adds only 3–8 mm rms over the circle run. This is the design of wb_ee_anchor_com: the arm does not try to cancel base error, and with the folded arm's few-centimetre workspace it could not cancel 100 mm anyway. Better absolute EE accuracy on the circle therefore has to come from the base loop, not the arm.
  • In z the arm dominates. The base holds height to 4–5 mm rms, so the EE's vertical error is mostly the task error: a 16–24 mm dip during each go-to-start, then up to ±20 mm through the run. In flights 3 and 4 the z task error is jagged rather than smooth: that is the joints' stick–slip (section 3.1) seen through the kinematics.
  • The task error is the arm's joint-tracking deficit seen through the kinematics. Mapping the measured q − q_d through the model's forward kinematics reproduces the logged task error with a residual of 1.6–5.6 mm rms and a correlation of 0.84–0.98 per axis (0.75–0.87 on flight 2's smaller lateral errors). Near the start pose one degree of q2/q3 split moves the EE only about 2 mm relative to the CoM, which is why 5–8° of joint error costs only 6–8 mm, and why the 20 N/m task spring pushes back with only ~0.1–0.3 N.
  • Yaw is tracked to about 1°. Heading error over the circle run is 0.75–1.13° rms in all four flights, peak 2.4°, while the reference turns through 15°/s. Every flight carries a small standing offset of +0.5° to +1.3°. The planner's independent FK heading agrees with the law's to 1.1–1.5° rms.

3. Open questions

Questions still being worked on. Each one ends with what to change or measure next.

3.1 Arm sinusoidal trajectory tracking

Status: open. Neither arm design tracks its joint sinusoid. The slow design (fold 60°, q2 = 30° ± 10°, 48 s) lags by 2.5–4 s and parks at 15–72 % of the commanded swing; the faster design (fold 55°, q2 = 25° ± 15°, 12 s and 6 s) covers the full swing but in stick–slip bursts that overshoot, and the q2/q3 error stays at 4–8° rms either way. The servos deliver what the arm controller asks to 0.006–0.041 N·m rms. The deficit is in the loop that commands the joints, and the ranked causes and fixes are below the data.

What causes the imperfect tracking of j2 and j3, from the highest priority down

  1. In whole-body DIRECT the joints have no loop of their own; they are held only by the end-effector impedance, which is weaker than the gearbox friction and almost absent in one joint direction. The law is an acceleration-level impedance: u3 = −J̄_3y⁻¹ Λ_y M_y⁻¹ K_y e_y + … with J̄_3y the dynamically-consistent Jacobian, so its joint stiffness is K_q ≈ M_ρ J⁻¹ (K_y/M_y) J — the model arm inertia times the task bandwidth squared (K_y/M_y = 20 s⁻², 4.5 rad/s). Evaluated exactly on the law's model at the circle poses (tools/arm_stiffness.py): 1.05 N·m/rad on j2 and 0.51 on j3 (damping 0.63 / 0.31 N·m·s/rad). Against the ground-calibrated breakaway, fc + µ|τ| ≈ 0.22 N·m on j2 at its 0.75 N·m load and ≈ 0.11 N·m on j3, that is ~12° of joint error before the spring can move either joint — the size of the measured peaks, 7.4–10.4° on j2 and 13.9–17.6° on j3. The j2/j3 block is also badly conditioned: its principal stiffnesses are 1.35 N·m/rad along (dq2, dq3) ∝ (0.86, 0.52) but only 0.20 N·m/rad along (0.52, −0.86), because moving the joints that way shifts the end-effector just 61 mm per radian (against 294). The end-effector task barely sees that direction, so it barely resists it — and the flights show exactly that: 94–99 % of the j2/j3 error energy lies along the soft direction in all four flights, with corr(e2, e3) = −0.86 to −0.99 (8.3 / 4.3 / 9.6 / 9.1° rms along it against 0.8–1.7° along the stiff one). That is why the end-effector task error stays near 1 cm while the joints wander 8–10°: a 9° soft-direction error moves the end-effector only ~1 cm. It also explains the static holds: j3 parked short of a static reference by −1.8 to −3.0° in every pre-run hold with 0.01–0.04° of ripple — a standing offset, not an oscillation. In the slow flights the joints creep and burst (measured peak speed 4.2–5.1°/s against a 1.3–1.6°/s reference) and lag by 2.5–4.0 s, realising 15–72 % of the swing. On the ground the PD+ law closes exactly the loop that is missing here — per-joint P (11.25 / 15.75 N·m/rad on j2/j3 in current mode), D and an integral, plus a stiction feed-forward — and a joint-space stiffness is the same in every joint direction, which is why the same arm tracked there.
  2. The friction feed-forward is a relay switched by the reference velocity, and it is 10–40 % too large in flight. Fig. 4 identifies the friction the servo actually pushes against beyond gravity: 0.141–0.186 N·m Coulomb on j2 against a feed-forward of 0.190–0.199 (0.74–0.93 of it), and 0.062–0.087 on j3 against 0.112–0.114 (0.55–0.76). Because the term is driven by the reference velocity with a 0.86°/s tanh width, it is fully on whenever the reference moves, whether or not the joint does. With cause 1 that produces two failures. Slow flights: the joint lags the reference by 2.5–4 s, so the feed-forward pushes against the real motion 40–49 % of the moving time. Fast flights: the joint sits stuck for 18–57 % of the moving time while the spring winds up, then the full feed-forward plus the wound-up spring act against a kinetic friction 10–40 % smaller than the feed-forward, and the joint bursts at 40–68°/s (4–7× the reference's peak) and overshoots the reference's extremes by up to +9.2° (j2) and −15.8° (j3); j3 sat on its +50° stop for 5.2 % of flight 4's run.
  3. The reference speed changes the failure mode, not its size. q2/q3 error rms is 4.2/7.3° at the 48 s period, 5.0/8.3° at 12 s and 5.1/7.7° at 6 s; a faster reference only saturates the feed-forward's tanh (0.86°/s width against 1.4, 8.7 and 17°/s peaks), swapping lag-and-park for stick–slip. At 6 s there is also no headroom left: the back-EMF term put j3's duty on its max_effort cap for 6 % of the run.
  4. Nothing the whole-body observer can do reaches this. Its arm channel filters at 0.5 rad/s, while the friction torque reverses sign every 3 s in flight 4 and every 12 s in flight 3 — 6–25× faster than the channel. It is not a cause of the deficit, but it cannot cover it either.

Ruled out by the same data: the servos (applied minus intended torque 0.006–0.041 N·m rms with zero mean, correlation 0.93–0.997 — the shortfall is in what is asked, not in what is delivered); torque headroom (peak applied torque 39–47 % of the j2 cap and 31–40 % of the j3 cap); the reference itself (the planner accepted every design from the 48 s to the 6 s period with peak joint torques of 0.82–0.87 N·m and unchanged base motion, and the base's circle error is 44–60 mm rms whatever the arm does); and the simulator's arm model (the identical 25° ± 15°, 12 s reference realised 103–109 % of its swing in the 2026-09-24 simulation check (campaign q2_sine_sim_20260924), whose friction model has no breakaway — so the swing is achievable and the difference is the hardware stiction).

Fixes for the next step, from the highest priority down

  1. Give the whole-body arm channel a joint-space loop. The provable form from the 2026-09-09 note is a joint potential K_y e_y + J_3y⁻ᵀ K_q e_q added to the task law (not the deleted PID on u3), tracking the planner's own consistent q_d. Raising wb_ky alone cannot get there: joint stiffness grows only with the task bandwidth, and the soft direction stays 6.6× softer than the stiff one. K_y 50 (flown in simulation) gives 2.6 / 1.3 N·m/rad but still only 0.51 along the soft direction (worst static friction error 46° → 18°); matching even the deleted 2 N·m/rad PID along the soft direction needs K_y ≈ 200 N/m (14 rad/s task bandwidth, flown only in the in-process rig without DDS), and matching the ground PD+ current-mode set needs K_y ≈ 1100 N/m (33 rad/s, 3× the 10 rad/s rotor pole). A K_y 50/20 flight is still worth doing as the quick A/B, but the joint term is the fix. It is also the only change that can remove the −1.8 to −3.0° standing offset on j3.
  2. Retune the friction feed-forward to the flight values and drive it from the measured velocity. j2 × 0.85 (µ 0.246 → 0.21) and j3 × 0.65 (fc 0.0575 → 0.037 N·m, µ 0.161 → 0.10), and switch its velocity input from the reference to the velocity observer that already feeds the back-EMF term, with a 0.05 rad/s width. It then opposes real motion only, so it cannot fight a lagging joint (40–49 % of the moving time in flights 1–2) or launch a stuck one (the 40–68°/s bursts).
  3. Add joint-space damping of about 1 N·m·s/rad on the arm channel (or raise wb_dy with item 1) to bound the burst speed and the +9.2 / −15.8° overshoots that put j3 on its stop.
  4. Keep the flight-3 design (fold 55°, q2 = 25° ± 15°, 12 s) for the A/B flights, and do not fly the 6 s period again until 1–3 are in: it put j3 on the +50° stop and its duty on the cap.
  5. Measure the breakaway (static) friction on the bench and put it in the simulator. The flight data cannot separate it from the kinetic value (the zero-velocity scatter is ±0.3–0.4 N·m on j2, larger than the 0.14–0.19 kinetic value), and the simulator's friction model, which has no breakaway, realised 103–112 % of the swing where the hardware realised 15–44 %, so it cannot be used to tune items 1–3.
  6. Score every A/B with this section's table (tools/arm_stats_table.py on the new bag): the targets are span realised → 100 %, stuck → 0 %, overshoot → 0, hold offset → the ground PD+'s sub-degree class, with applied − intended staying at its present 0.006–0.041 N·m.

4.1 Why flight 1 drifted at the end of lap 2

Cause: a 2.85 s pause in the motion-capture stream at heading 135°, which the ground-station processor turned into a frozen but fresh-stamped pose that EKF2 fused. The fused estimate froze with it, the circle reference kept moving, and the whole-body law pushed the real vehicle to close a tracking error that only existed in the estimate.

Measured sequence:

  1. 75.51 s: the Motive (VRPN) stream into the ground station pauses (broadcast_position_velocity: NO DATA at 75.55 s; the next subsection shows where the pause came from). The vehicle is at (0.516, 0.213, 1.066) m, yaw 135°, 39.6 s into the 52 s run (1.57 laps).
  2. The ground-station processor falls back to republishing the last pose with fresh header stamps (169 identical poses over 2.85 s at 60 Hz, twist = 0). The estimator forwards every /mocap message to fmu/in/vehicle_visual_odometry with fixed 1e-4 variances and no staleness check (indoor_state_estimator.cpp), so EKF2 sees 162 unique, monotone timestamp_samples carrying the same position and zero velocity, and fuses them as valid. cs_ev_pos stays 1, no dead-reckoning flag, no reset, no failsafe. The fused estimate moves only 62 mm in 2.6 s.
  3. The reference travels 365 mm in that time. The law's error grows to (+146, −190) mm and |e_R| to 0.18 (10°); u1 stays at hover. It is commanding a lateral acceleration of up to ~2 m/s² toward the reference — on a vehicle that is actually free to move.
  4. 78.09 s: the operator reverts to SAFETY (no watchdog line in /rosout; the 0.75 m drift guard measures against the frozen estimate, so it could not fire). 78.36–78.47 s: the stream resumes, and the drone is 1.35 m away, at (−0.14, 1.41) m, moving at ~1 m/s along the circle tangent — exactly the direction the law had been pushing.
  5. EKF2 now rejects the re-acquired position for 1.2 s (its gate works; the estimate stays at the frozen point while the vehicle is 1.4 m away), then resets at 79.67 s (reset_counter 13→14). At the same instant the pilot switches to Stabilized (nav_state 14→15), so the SAFETY setpoint (−8°/+7° toward home) is never flown. The vehicle coasts at 0.95 m/s, descends, and touches down at 80.8 s, 3.7 m from the freeze point, with a 58° tilt transient (tumbled), and is disarmed at 81.1 s.

Supporting evidence that it is the feed, not the law: the two 108 ms pauses (55.07 s in flight 1 and 37.19 s in flight 2, at yaw −172° and 139°) were absorbed by EKF2 with ≤ 25 mm of estimate error and no visible effect on the flight; the law's outputs were unremarkable until the estimate stopped moving; and the fly-away direction and speed match the integrated position-loop force.

Where the freeze came from: Motive's output stream paused

Not the estimator, and not the WiFi. The stream of poses from Motive into the ground station paused, while Motive was still measuring the drone correctly. The evidence points at Motive's output pipeline falling behind and dropping frames at one range of drone headings, rather than at the rigid body being lost.

The data path. Motive PC → VRPN → ground-station computer (vrpn_mocap client, then broadcast_position_velocity, which publishes /uav_0/mocap) → WiFi → drone (estimator → EKF2). The bag's own clock stamps place the processor, both ground stations and the recorder on the ground-station computer: their log messages reach the recorder in 0.2 ms, while every node on the drone arrives with a constant −3.6 ms clock offset. Earlier versions of this report put the processor on the drone; that was wrong.

  • Not the estimator. The estimator only consumes /uav_0/mocap. The pause was detected upstream: the ground-station processor logged FEEDBACK LOST!! NO DATA: /vrpn_mocap/uav_0/pose at 75.548 s, 38 ms after the last frame. From then on it republished the last pose at 60 Hz with fresh stamps and zero velocity; its source code does exactly this on loss. The estimator forwarded what it received; its fault is only that it cannot tell a frozen pose from a hover.
  • Not the drone's WiFi, and not the ground station's network. Through every pause, the drone's 250 Hz law debug and 400 Hz attitude reached the recorder without interruption (longest interval 7.8 ms against 5.1 ms normally). The drone also kept receiving the frozen republishes and forwarding them to PX4 (fig. 7c). Drone-to-ground latency during the long pause was −3.72 ms median against −3.59 ms before. The operator's Safety command also reached the drone at 78.09 s, inside the pause.
  • Not a lost rigid body. The first frame to arrive after the long pause, at 78.36 s, carried a pose the drone's own IMU places at 75.64 s, matching to about 1 mm (fig. 7a). The dead-reckoning method is accurate to 38–116 mm over the same 2.85 s span elsewhere in the flight. So Motive was still measuring the drone correctly 0.13 s after deliveries stopped, and that measurement took 2.72 s to arrive. The short pauses show the same thing more clearly (fig. 7b). After each ~108 ms gap, frames resume at the normal 8 ms cadence but continue from where the drone was when the pause began. They run about 100 ms late for five frames, then a single ~13 mm skip returns the stream to live. There are no orientation snaps around any pause. Snaps are what Motive produces when markers are occluded, per the lab's July VRPN analysis.
  • It depends on where the drone is pointing. All three in-flight pauses happened at headings between 135° and 188° (135°, 139°, −172°), a sector the drone occupied for 7 % of its DIRECT time in both flights (fig. 7d). The chance of that happening by coincidence is about 4 in 10,000. A network or computer hiccup does not care about the drone's heading; Motive's workload at a particular viewpoint does.

Late frames delivered at the camera's own cadence, followed by a skip back to live, is how a real-time pipeline behaves when it falls behind and then drops frames to catch up. A network buffer releasing a backlog would deliver it in a sub-millisecond burst instead. The best-supported source is therefore Motive's processing on the Motive PC (reconstruction, marker labelling and rigid-body solving), stalling at those viewpoints: the drone stayed visible, but its poses left Motive late, and during the long pause not at all. Two alternatives this bag cannot fully exclude are a stall in the Motive PC's network output and a stall of the ground station's VRPN client process. Neither explains the heading dependence.

To confirm on the next flight, without changing anything in the control loop:

  • Record /vrpn_mocap/uav_0/pose on the ground station as it is (done in flights 3 and 4, next subsection) (best-effort QoS; its stamps are the ground station's receive time). For Motive's own frame times, run a second, recording-only vrpn_mocap client in its own namespace with use_vrpn_timestamps: true. Do not switch that option on the client the processor uses: the processor computes velocity from those stamps, so it would change what EKF2 receives. Arrival time minus Motive's frame time then separates Motive-side delay from network delay.
  • Stream and record every rigid body. If all bodies pause together, Motive's whole frame stalled; if only uav_0 does, it is that asset's solve.
  • Hover the drone at headings 135°–190° near (0.5, 0.2, 1.0) m and watch Motive's latency and dropped-frame indicators and the Motive PC's CPU. Look for what that viewpoint adds: the arm or gripper markers, or reflections in that part of the volume.
  • Check the Motive PC's logs at these times (ground-station clock, local time, 24 Sep): flight 1 at 12:03:23.74 (108 ms) and 12:03:44.18 (2.85 s); flight 2 at 12:05:47.73 and 12:05:47.82 (on the ground) and 12:06:24.24 (108 ms).

Did the Motive stream stall again in flights 3 and 4?

No. /vrpn_mocap/uav_0/pose was recorded in both afternoon flights: 120.0 Hz, 8.0 ms median spacing, longest interval 13 ms (F3) and 19 ms (F4), zero gaps over 20 ms, zero identical consecutive poses, zero orientation snaps, zero position steps over 5 cm, and no NO DATA line from the processor. Every VRPN frame produced a processed frame (8360 vs 8359 in F3). The drone flew through the 135°–188° heading sector that paused the morning stream, with no effect (the per-heading skip rate is 0–1 % everywhere, all single 12–19 ms frames). Whatever stalled Motive's output at 12:03 did not recur at 16:56 or 17:21; it remains unexplained, and the processor's stale republishing is still the thing to fix so that the next pause cannot become a frozen estimate.

What to change before the next circle

  • Stop republishing a stale pose as fresh (broadcast_position_velocity on the ground-station computer): on NO DATA, either stop publishing or keep the last frame's original stamp. Belt and braces, drop EV samples in the estimator whose pose is bit-identical to the previous one or older than ~50 ms. EKF2 will then dead-reckon on the IMU, cs_ev_pos drops, and the client's feedback-loss failsafe (1.0 s) hands control to SAFETY with a moving estimate instead of a frozen one.
  • Guard in the DIRECT client for a frozen feed: N identical odometry samples (or a stale mocap status) = feedback lost, independent of the estimator.
  • Find why Motive stalls at that viewpoint with the checks above, and hand-track a full yaw circle at flight height before the next circle flight.
  • Consider EKF2's EV timeout/gate parameters (EKF2_EV_DELAY, EKF2_EVP_GATE): with the current bridge the frozen input is indistinguishable from a hover, so the parameter route alone will not catch it.

  • EKF2 fusion — the largest gain, and the one that carried the flight. The law's velocity feedback is 8× quieter than 0921 and cleaner than 0918's 60 Hz feed; the deadbeat estimate and the collective's high-frequency content dropped 7–17×; |e_R| in hold is the lowest recorded; no mocap step was consumed (the two 108 ms pauses became ≤ 25 mm of estimate error). Costs: ~20 ms of lag and 4–9 mm rms offset against mocap, and the stale-input failure mode of section 4.1, which the raw path would have shown as a frozen pose too (with the same result). Fusion did its job; the broadcaster did not.
  • Ethernet PX4↔Orin link — works. Timesync round trip 3.7 → 0.45 ms, clock-offset drift over a flight 39 ms (0918) → 0.25 ms. 400 Hz topics arrive with 4.2 ms p99 spacing, i.e. no queuing; the reference and actuator streams are unchanged at 100 / 250 Hz.
  • CPU isolation — modest, positive. Law tick p99 5.16–5.27 ms vs 5.33–5.81, worst tick 12.2 vs 16.9 ms, ticks over 8 ms 3 vs 17 (0918). The reference stream's 100 Hz p99 is unchanged (11.7 ms). Nothing in these bags was timing-limited before either.
  • 400 Hz sensor_combined / vehicle_attitude — clean timing, but the raw gyro now passes twice the vibration bandwidth into the rate loop. sensor_combined is the unfiltered integrated gyro; at 400 Hz its 2.5 ms window averages half as much. The gyro's above-15 Hz power fraction rose from 32–76 % to 86–89 % with lines at 136–158 Hz (rotor harmonics), the motor commands' high-frequency rms rose 40 %, body-torque std on y rose 35 %, and the arm torque's tick-to-tick noise rose 40 %. Attitude quality did not suffer (PX4 roll/pitch std unchanged, 0 % power above 5 Hz), and nothing rang, so this is an actuator-wear and heating cost, not a stability one. Options: keep 400 Hz for vehicle_attitude and feed the law's rate from PX4's filtered vehicle_angular_velocity (IMU_GYRO_CUTOFF), or low-pass ω in the client (~40 Hz, well above the 4.9 rad/s attitude loop), or return sensor_combined to 200 Hz.

5. Traps recorded while doing this

  • The fused estimator stamps local_position/odom on PX4's clock (header − receive ≈ −2.5e9 s); window it on receive time.
  • Both ROS bags read as "NO DATA" episodes in /rosout, but only the /mocap identical-pose runs tell the duration; mocap_status read mocap_normal throughout, including the 2.85 s pause.
  • joint_states effort is in Dynamixel current counts, not N·m; the law's commanded torque is wb_control_debug[13..16].
  • nav_state 15 in this PX4 build is Stabilized: the pilot took over 1.6 s after the revert, so the SAFETY-mode recovery was never flown.
